::: {.front card-id="matroid-independent-bases-rank" tags="definition comparison example" cite="[@Ale15, Chapter 3]"}
Define a finite matroid by independent sets. Recover bases, circuits, and rank; give a vector example and the uniform matroid.
:::

::: {.back card-id="matroid-independent-bases-rank"}
$M=(E,\mathcal I)$, $E$ finite:

- $\varnothing\in\mathcal I$; $J\subseteq I\in\mathcal I\Rightarrow J\in\mathcal I$.
- Exchange: $I,J\in\mathcal I$, $|I|<|J|\Rightarrow\exists e\in J\setminus I$ with $I\cup\{e\}\in\mathcal I$.

| Object | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Basis | Maximal independent subset of $E$ |
| Circuit | Minimal dependent subset |
| Rank | $r(A)=\max\{|I|:I\subseteq A,\ I\in\mathcal I\}$ |

Columns $(v_e)_{e\in E}$ of a matrix define a representable matroid: $I$ independent iff $(v_e)_{e\in I}$ are linearly independent. The field is part of a representation.

$U_{r,n}$: $|E|=n$, $\mathcal I=\{I\subseteq E:|I|\le r\}$. For $U_{2,3}$ use $e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2$; bases are the pairs, and the whole set is its only circuit.
:::

::: {.front card-id="matroid-flats-components" tags="definition example"}
Define closure, flat, loop, coloop, and connectedness of a matroid. Interpret them for a spanning list of vectors.
:::

::: {.back card-id="matroid-flats-components"}
| Notion | Criterion |
| --- | --- |
| Closure | $\operatorname{cl}(A)=\{e:r(A\cup\{e\})=r(A)\}$ |
| Flat | $A=\operatorname{cl}(A)$ |
| Loop | $r(\{e\})=0$ |
| Coloop | $e\in B$ for every basis $B$ |
| Connected | No partition $E=A\sqcup B$ into nonempty sets with $r(E)=r(A)+r(B)$ |

For vectors: closure adds exactly the listed vectors in their span; a loop is a zero vector; a coloop is outside the span of all the other vectors. Connected components are the equivalence classes generated by membership in a common circuit; isolated elements give singleton components.

Example: $e_1,e_2,e_1+e_2$ form a connected matroid. Adding $e_3$ in $k^3$ adds a coloop and a separate component. For a hyperplane arrangement $H_i=\ker f_i$, use the vector matroid of the linear forms $f_i$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="matroid-minors-duality" tags="definition comparison application"}
Define restriction, deletion, contraction, and duality of matroids. How do restriction and contraction act on a hyperplane arrangement?
:::

::: {.back card-id="matroid-minors-duality"}
$M$ on $E$, $A\subseteq E$:

| Operation | Rank function or bases |
| --- | --- |
| Restriction $M|A$ | $r_{M|A}(B)=r_M(B)$ for $B\subseteq A$ |
| Deletion $M\setminus A$ | $M|(E\setminus A)$ |
| Contraction $M/A$ | $r_{M/A}(B)=r_M(B\cup A)-r_M(A)$ |
| Dual $M^*$ | Bases $E\setminus B$ for bases $B$ of $M$ |

Thus $r_{M^*}(B)=|B|-r_M(E)+r_M(E\setminus B)$ and $(M\setminus A)^*=M^*/A$.

For vectors, contraction projects the remaining vectors modulo $\operatorname{span}(v_a:a\in A)$. For $H_i=\ker f_i\subset V$, it restricts the remaining forms to $\bigcap_{a\in A}H_a$. A form restricting to zero becomes a loop. Restriction keeps selected hyperplanes, with the common kernel removed if an essential arrangement is wanted.

Example: $U_{2,3}/\{3\}=U_{1,2}$, whereas deleting $3$ gives $U_{2,2}$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="matroid-graphic-regular" tags="definition comparison example"}
Compare graphic, cographic, and regular matroids. Define total unimodularity and give a reusable representation.
:::

::: {.back card-id="matroid-graphic-regular"}
For a finite graph $\Gamma$ (loops and multiple edges allowed):

- Graphic matroid $M(\Gamma)$: independent edge sets are forests; circuits are graph cycles.
- Cographic matroid $M^*(\Gamma)$: dual to $M(\Gamma)$; circuits are minimal nonempty edge cuts.
- Regular matroid: representable over every field; equivalently, represented by a totally unimodular integer matrix.
- Totally unimodular: **every square minor** is $0$, $1$, or $-1$.

An oriented vertex-edge incidence matrix, with one vertex row removed per connected component, is totally unimodular and represents $M(\Gamma)$. Its kernel represents the cycle space; the coordinate functionals on this kernel represent the cographic matroid.

Triangle: $M(\Gamma)=U_{2,3}$, $M^*(\Gamma)=U_{1,3}$. Graphic and cographic matroids are regular; regular does not mean graphic.
:::

::: {.front card-id="matroid-polytopes-hypersimplex" tags="definition formula example"}
Define the independent-set and base polytopes of a matroid. Recover the hypersimplex, its weighted version, and the polytope inequalities from rank.
:::

::: {.back card-id="matroid-polytopes-hypersimplex"}
$M$ on $[n]$, rank $r$; $e_I=\sum_{i\in I}e_i$, $x(A)=\sum_{i\in A}x_i$:
$$P_{\mathcal I}(M)=\operatorname{conv}\{e_I:I\text{ independent}\}
=\{x\ge0:x(A)\le r(A)\ \forall A\subseteq[n]\},$$
$$P(M)=\operatorname{conv}\{e_B:B\text{ a basis}\}
=P_{\mathcal I}(M)\cap\{x([n])=r\}.$$
For $U_{r,n}$:
$$\Delta(r,n)=\{0\le x_i\le1,\ \sum x_i=r\}.$$
For weights $0<b_i\le1$, $\sum b_i>r$:
$$\Delta_b(r,n)=\{0\le x_i\le b_i,\ \sum x_i=r\}.$$
Weighted matroid polytopes are nonempty intersections $P(M)\cap\Delta_b$.

$\Delta(2,3)$ is the triangle with vertices $(1,1,0),(1,0,1),(0,1,1)$. More generally $\dim P(M)=n-c(M)$, where $c(M)$ counts connected components, including singleton loops and coloops.
:::

::: {.front card-id="matroid-subdivision-realization" tags="definition construction example" cite="[@Ale15, Chapters 3–4]"}
What is a matroid subdivision? Relate a realizable matroid polytope to a torus orbit in a Grassmannian.
:::

::: {.back card-id="matroid-subdivision-realization"}
A polyhedral subdivision of $P$ is a finite collection of polytopes covering $P$, closed under faces, with every intersection a face of both. A **matroid subdivision** of $\Delta(r,n)$ has matroid base polytopes as its cells.

For $V\in\operatorname{Gr}(r,n)$, the nonzero Plücker coordinates $p_I(V)$ are the bases of its matroid. Under the coordinate torus $T=(\mathbb G_m)^n/\mathbb G_m$:
$$P(V)=\operatorname{conv}\{e_I:p_I(V)\ne0\}.$$
This is the weight polytope of the Plücker-polarized orbit closure $\overline{T\cdot V}$. A representable rank-two matroid with parallel first and second columns has $p_{12}=0$: its polytope omits $e_1+e_2$.

“Matroid subdivision” is combinatorial; realizability of its cells over a given field and coherence of the subdivision are additional conditions.
:::
