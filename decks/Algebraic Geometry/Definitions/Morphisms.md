::: {.front card-id="card-2fc77d27d9c19b46"}
What is a **separated morphism**?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-2fc77d27d9c19b46"}
A morphism $f:X\to S$ is **separated** iff the diagonal $\Delta_{X/S}:X\to X\times_S X$ is a closed immersion, i.e. it is affine (preimages of affine opens are affine) and the map $\mathcal O_{X\times_S X}\to(\Delta_{X/S})_*\mathcal O_X$ is surjective on stalks.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-17e3b6dd4ac0258d"}
What is a **quasicompact morphism**? When is a scheme **quasicompact over a base**?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-17e3b6dd4ac0258d"}
A morphism $f:X\to Y$ is **quasicompact** iff for every open $U\subseteq Y$ such that every open cover of $U$ has a finite subcover, the preimage $f^{-1}(U)$ has the same property. Equivalently, some open affine cover $\{V_i\}$ of $Y$ has every $f^{-1}(V_i)$ quasicompact.

A scheme $X$ over $S$ is **quasicompact over $S$** iff its structure morphism $X\to S$ is quasicompact. For $S=\Spec\ZZ$, this is the usual absolute notion of a quasicompact scheme.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-67acbfbbf098e7b7"}
What is a locally finitely presented morphism?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-67acbfbbf098e7b7"}
A morphism $f:X\to Y$ is **locally of finite presentation** iff for every $x\in X$ there are affine opens $U=\Spec A\subseteq X$ and $V=\Spec R\subseteq Y$ with $x\in U$, $f(U)\subseteq V$, and $A\cong R[x_1,\cdots,x_n]/(f_1,\cdots,f_m)$ for some $n,m$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-c35dccf24f080a71"}
What is a finitely presented morphism?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-c35dccf24f080a71"}
A morphism $f:X\to Y$ is **finitely presented** iff it is locally of finite presentation (every point has affine neighborhoods $\Spec A\to\Spec R$ with $A$ a finitely presented $R$-algebra), quasicompact (preimages of quasicompact opens are quasicompact), and quasi-separated (the diagonal is quasicompact).
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-525d10c2757e41de" tags="unsorted"}
What is a proper morphism? What are some consequences?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-525d10c2757e41de"}
A morphism $f:X\to Y$ is **proper** iff it is separated (the diagonal $X\to X\times_Y X$ is a closed immersion), of finite type (every point has an affine neighborhood $\Spec A$ mapping into an affine open $\Spec R$ with $A$ a finitely generated $R$-algebra, and $f$ is quasicompact), and universally closed (every base change $X\times_Y Y'\to Y'$ is a closed map).

Equivalently, for $f$ of finite type and quasi-separated, the valuative criterion holds: for every valuation ring $R$ with fraction field $K$, every commutative square $\Spec K\to X$ over $\Spec R\to Y$ has a unique lift $\Spec R\to X$.

Consequences:

- A variety $X$ over a field is **complete** if its structure morphism $X\to\Spec k$ is proper.
- Fibers of a proper morphism are complete.
- Inverse images of complete subvarieties of $Y$ are complete.
- Any morphism obtained by base change from a proper morphism is proper.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-bf7f67bffb5a2228"}
What is an affine morphism?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-bf7f67bffb5a2228"}
A morphism $f:X\to Y$ is **affine** iff for every affine open $U\subseteq Y$, the preimage $f^{-1}(U)$ is affine.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-50c3a5b5a43dbd56" tags="definition"}
What is a **finite morphism**?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-50c3a5b5a43dbd56"}
A morphism $f:X\to Y$ is **finite** iff for every affine open $\Spec A\subseteq Y$, the preimage is $\Spec B$ with $B$ a finitely generated $A$-module. Equivalently, $f$ is affine and locally given by finite ring maps.

Being finitely generated as a module is stronger than being finitely generated as an algebra: $k[x]$ is generated by one element as a $k$-algebra but has infinite rank as a $k$-module. Thus finite implies finite type (finitely generated as an algebra) but not conversely.

Equivalently, $f$ is proper (separated, of finite type, and universally closed) and quasi-finite (locally of finite type with finite fibers).
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-b00d12404bc086fb"}
What is a **flat** morphism?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-b00d12404bc086fb"}
A module $M$ over a ring $A$ is **flat** iff tensoring with $M$ is exact. A morphism $f:X\to Y$ is **flat at $x\in X$** iff $\mathcal O_{X,x}$ is flat over $\mathcal O_{Y,f(x)}$; $f$ is flat iff it is flat at every $x\in X$. For a morphism of projective varieties, this is equivalent to the induced maps on stalks being flat ring maps.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-e504a4da2d4624f3"}
What is a **smooth morphism** of relative dimension $n$?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-e504a4da2d4624f3"}
(Hartshorne, for schemes of finite type over $k$) A morphism $f:X\to Y$ is **smooth of relative dimension $n$** iff:

1. $f$ is flat (tensoring with $\mathcal O_{X,x}$ is exact for every $x\in X$);
2. $\dim X'=\dim Y'+n$ for all irreducible components $X'\subseteq X$ and $Y'\subseteq Y$ with $f(X')\subseteq Y'$;
3. $\dim_{\kappa(x)}(\Omega_{X/Y}\otimes\kappa(x))=n$ for every $x\in X$, where $\Omega_{X/Y}$ is the sheaf of relative Kähler differentials.

For $Y$ irreducible and $f$ flat, condition 2 is equivalent to every irreducible component of every fiber $X_y$ of $f$ having dimension $n$.

For $X$ integral, condition 3 can be replaced by: $\Omega_{X/Y}$ is locally free of rank $n$.

Smooth morphisms are stable under base change and composition.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-e9ced257a3fb0b70"}
What is an **unramified** morphism?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-e9ced257a3fb0b70"}
A morphism $f:X\to Y$ is **unramified** iff it is locally of finite presentation (every $x\in X$ has affine neighborhoods $\Spec A\subseteq X$ and $\Spec R\subseteq Y$ with $f(\Spec A)\subseteq\Spec R$ and $A$ a finitely presented $R$-algebra) and for every $x\in X$, the induced map $f^\sharp:\mathcal O_{Y,f(x)}\to\mathcal O_{X,x}$ satisfies $f^\sharp(\mathfrak m_{f(x)})\mathcal O_{X,x}=\mathfrak m_x$, and $\kappa(x)/\kappa(f(x))$ is a separable algebraic extension.

Equivalently, $f$ is locally of finite presentation and $\Omega_{X/Y}=0$.

Equivalently, $f$ is locally of finite presentation and the diagonal $\Delta_{X/Y}$ is an open immersion (an isomorphism onto an open subscheme).

For a finite (affine with finite module) morphism of smooth curves, there are $e_p\in\ZZ_{\ge0}$ such that $f^\sharp(\mathfrak m_{f(p)})\mathcal O_{X,p}=\mathfrak m_p^{e_p}$, and $f$ is unramified iff $e_p=1$ for all $p\in X$. The integer $e_p$ is the ramification index.

Flat + unramified = etale.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-a484edc8e6016938"}
What is an **etale** morphism $f:X\to Y$ of $k$-schemes?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-a484edc8e6016938"}
A morphism $f:X\to Y$ is **etale** iff it is flat (tensoring with $\mathcal O_{X,x}$ is exact at every $x\in X$), locally of finite presentation (every point has affine neighborhoods $\Spec A\to\Spec R$ with $A$ a finitely presented $R$-algebra), and unramified (the maximal ideal of $\mathcal O_{Y,f(x)}$ generates the maximal ideal of $\mathcal O_{X,x}$, and the residue extension is separable algebraic). Equivalently, it is flat, locally of finite presentation, and $\Omega_{X/Y}=0$. Equivalently, it is smooth of relative dimension $0$.

For schemes of finite type over a field, locally of finite presentation is automatic, so etale means flat and unramified.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-c79260140f5f0dbd"}
When is a morphism of schemes an immersion?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-c79260140f5f0dbd"}
A morphism $\iota:X\to Z$ is an **immersion** iff it factors as $X\xrightarrow{j}U\to Z$, where $j$ is a closed immersion (affine and the map $\mathcal O_U\to j_*\mathcal O_X$ is surjective on stalks) and $U\to Z$ is an open immersion (an isomorphism onto an open subscheme). Equivalently, $X$ is a closed subscheme of an open subscheme of $Z$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-86f3fc66ea0fc489"}
What is a **closed immersion** of schemes?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-86f3fc66ea0fc489"}
A morphism $f:X\to Y$ is a **closed immersion** iff it is affine (preimages of affine opens are affine) and the map $f^\sharp:\mathcal O_Y\to f_*\mathcal O_X$ is surjective on stalks. Equivalently, on every affine open $\Spec B\subseteq Y$, the induced ring map $B\to A$ is surjective, where $f^{-1}(\Spec B)=\Spec A$.
:::
