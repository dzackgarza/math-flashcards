::: {.front card-id="tropical-valuation-initial-basis" tags="definition comparison example"}
Define tropicalization using valuations and initial ideals. Distinguish a tropical basis from generators of an ideal; compute a tropical line.
:::

::: {.back card-id="tropical-valuation-initial-basis"}
Use the minimum convention. Let $K$ be algebraically closed with a nontrivial real valuation $v$, and $X=V(I)\subset(K^*)^n$. Then
$$\operatorname{Trop}(X)=\overline{\{(v(x_1),\ldots,v(x_n)):x\in X(K)\}}\subset\mathbb R^n.$$
For $f=\sum a_ux^u$, retain terms minimizing $v(a_u)+\langle w,u\rangle$, rescale to valuation zero and reduce to obtain $\operatorname{in}_w(f)$; use a valued extension when necessary to realize $w$. The initial ideal is generated by initial forms of **all** $f\in I$.
$$w\in\operatorname{Trop}(X)\iff\operatorname{in}_w(I)\text{ contains no monomial}
\iff\forall f\in I,\ \min_u(v(a_u)+\langle w,u\rangle)\text{ attained at least twice}.$$
A finite subset $F\subset I$ is a tropical basis if $\operatorname{Trop}(X)=\bigcap_{f\in F}\operatorname{Trop}(V(f))$. An arbitrary generating set can give a strictly larger intersection, a tropical prevariety.

For $1+x+y$, the minimum of $0,w_1,w_2$ occurs twice on rays generated by $(1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1)$.

Reference: Caminata–Giansiracusa–Moon–Schaffler, *Point configurations, phylogenetic trees, and dissimilarity vectors*, §§2–3.
:::

::: {.front card-id="tropical-balancing-compactification" tags="definition comparison example"}
State tropical balancing and define a tropical compactification. Which conditions go beyond choosing a fan containing the tropicalization?
:::

::: {.back card-id="tropical-balancing-compactification"}
For a pure-dimensional rational polyhedral complex with integer weights $m_\sigma$ on its maximal cells, balancing at a codimension-one face $\tau$ means
$$\sum_{\sigma\supset\tau}m_\sigma u_{\sigma/\tau}=0
\quad\text{in }N/N_\tau,$$
where $u_{\sigma/\tau}$ is the primitive integral outward direction modulo the saturated tangent lattice $N_\tau$. The three primitive rays of a tropical line have weight one and sum to zero.

For a closed subvariety $X$ of a torus $T$ over an algebraically closed trivially valued field, let $\overline X\subset Y_\Sigma$ be its closure in a toric variety. It is a **tropical compactification** if $\overline X$ is proper and the multiplication map
$$T\times\overline X\to Y_\Sigma,\qquad(t,x)\mapsto t\cdot x$$
is faithfully flat. A **schön compactification** requires this map smooth. Proper closure and support conditions alone do not state the flatness requirement.

References: Gallardo–Schaffler, *Mustafin degenerations of six points and self-duality*, §2; Tevelev, *Compactifications of subvarieties of tori*.
:::

::: {.front card-id="tropical-grassmannian-tree-metric" tags="definition comparison example"}
Relate the tropical Grassmannian $\operatorname{Trop}\operatorname{Gr}(2,n)$ to tree metrics. State the four-point condition and explain the sign convention.
:::

::: {.back card-id="tropical-grassmannian-tree-metric"}
Tropical Grassmannian: tropicalization of the Plücker ideal on the locus where all Plücker coordinates are nonzero, modulo the common-coordinate line in tropical projective space.

For a metric $d=(d_{ij})$ on $n$ labels, a tree with nonnegative edge lengths realizes $d$ iff for every distinct $i,j,k,l$ the maximum of
$$d_{ij}+d_{kl},\quad d_{ik}+d_{jl},\quad d_{il}+d_{jk}$$
occurs at least twice. This is the four-point condition. In the minimum convention, the tropical Plücker coordinates are $w_{ij}=-d_{ij}$. General tropical points allow arbitrary pendant lengths; changing them adds the lineality vector $(a_i+a_j)_{ij}$.

Example: a four-leaf tree with pendant lengths one and an internal edge of length $a>0$ separating $\{1,2\}$ from $\{3,4\}$ has
$$d_{12}+d_{34}=4,\qquad d_{13}+d_{24}=d_{14}+d_{23}=4+2a.$$
The two largest sums agree. At $a=0$ the internal edge contracts and all three sums agree.

Reference: [Caminata–Giansiracusa–Moon–Schaffler, §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02629).
:::

::: {.front card-id="tropical-building-mustafin" tags="definition construction example"}
Define the lattice vertices and apartments of the Bruhat–Tits building of $\operatorname{PGL}(V)$. Construct a Mustafin degeneration and compute the two-lattice case for $\dim V=2$.
:::

::: {.back card-id="tropical-building-mustafin"}
Let $R$ be a DVR with uniformizer $t$, fraction field $K$, and $V=K^n$. A lattice is a free rank-$n$ $R$-submodule spanning $V$. Vertices are homothety classes $[L]$, $L\sim aL$ for $a\in K^*$. A simplex is a chain between $tL$ and $L$, modulo homothety. A basis $(e_i)$ gives the apartment
$$\left[\bigoplus_i Rt^{a_i}e_i\right],\qquad(a_i)\in\mathbb Z^n/\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1).$$
For finitely many vertices $\Gamma$, the Mustafin variety is the scheme-theoretic closure of the diagonal $\mathbb P(V)$ in $\prod_{[L]\in\Gamma}\mathbb P(L)$, using the convention that projective spaces parametrize lines. It is flat over $R$ with generic fiber $\mathbb P(V)$.

For $L_0=Re_1+Re_2$, $L_1=Rte_1+Re_2$, coordinates $[X:Y]$, $[U:V]$ satisfy
$$XV=tUY.$$
The special fiber $XV=0$ is two copies of $\mathbb P^1$ meeting in one node. Thus neighboring lattice classes give the familiar nodal degeneration.

Reference: [Gallardo–Schaffler, §2.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35060).
:::
