::: {.front card-id="cones-nonpolyhedral-abelian-example" tags="example application"}
Give a concrete nonpolyhedral nef cone and distinguish irrational boundary rays from merely having infinitely many rational extremal rays.
:::

::: {.back card-id="cones-nonpolyhedral-abelian-example"}
Let $A=E\times E$ over $\mathbb C$ with $\operatorname{End}(E)=\mathbb Z$. Under the product principal polarization, $N^1(A)_{\mathbb R}$ identifies with symmetric real $2\times2$ matrices. Ample classes correspond to positive definite matrices, nef classes to positive semidefinite matrices:
$$\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&c\end{pmatrix}\text{ nef}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a\ge0,\ c\ge0,\ ac-b^2\ge0.$$
The rank-one rays $vv^t$ are extremal. They form a continuous family; choosing $v=(1,\sqrt2)$ gives an irrational ray. Thus the cone is not polyhedral.

Infinite rational extremal rays can instead occur for plane blowups at very general points. Neither behavior contradicts the cone theorem: it controls the $K$-negative region, and here $K_A=0$.

Reference: [Totaro, Algebraic surfaces and hyperbolic geometry](https://library.slmath.org/books/Book59/files/68totaro.pdf).
:::

::: {.front card-id="cones-mori-chamber-surface-example" tags="application"}
What is a Mori chamber decomposition? Describe the big-cone chambers on $\operatorname{Bl}_p\mathbb P^2$.
:::

::: {.back card-id="cones-mori-chamber-surface-example"}
For a Mori dream space, the effective cone has a finite rational polyhedral decomposition according to the birational model defined by section rings and the fixed divisorial part. The movable cone is assembled from nef cones of small $\mathbb Q$-factorial modifications; a wall can change the model or the fixed part.

For $D=aH-bE$ in the big cone, $a>0$ and $a>b$. If $0<b<a$, then $D$ is ample and its model is the blowup itself. If $b<0$, write
$$D=aH+(-b)E.$$
This is the Zariski decomposition: $E$ is fixed and the positive part is pulled back from $\mathbb P^2$, so the section-ring model is $\mathbb P^2$. The wall $b=0$ is generated by $H$ and gives the blowdown. The boundary $a=b>0$ gives a ruling and is outside the big cone.
:::

::: {.front card-id="definition-divisor-volume" tags="definition application"}
Define the volume of a Cartier divisor on a projective variety. When does it equal a top self-intersection, and what does positivity detect?
:::

::: {.back card-id="definition-divisor-volume"}
For a Cartier divisor $D$ on an integral projective $n$-fold with $n>0$,
$$\operatorname{vol}_X(D)=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{n!\,h^0(X,\mathcal O_X(mD))}{m^n}.$$
It measures the leading asymptotic growth of sections. It depends only on the numerical class, is homogeneous of degree $n$, and is positive exactly when $D$ is big. For a nef divisor, $\operatorname{vol}(D)=D^n$.

Nefness matters: on $\operatorname{Bl}_p\mathbb P^2$, let $H$ be the pullback of a line and $E$ the exceptional curve. Then $D=H+E$ has fixed part $mE$ in every $|mD|$, so $h^0(mD)=\binom{m+2}{2}$ and $\operatorname{vol}(D)=1$, although $D^2=0$. For rational divisors, define volume by clearing denominators and using homogeneity.
:::

::: {.front card-id="definition-nef-numerical-dimension" tags="definition application"}
Define the numerical dimension of a nef divisor. Compare it with Iitaka dimension for $\mathcal O(1,0)$ on $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$ and for degree-zero line bundles on an elliptic curve.
:::

::: {.back card-id="definition-nef-numerical-dimension"}
For a nef divisor $D$ on a smooth projective $n$-fold and an ample divisor $A$,
$$\nu(D)=\max\{k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}:D^k\cdot A^{n-k}>0\}.$$
It is independent of $A$ and depends only on the numerical class. It measures numerical positivity; $\nu(D)=n$ exactly when $D$ is big. This ordinary-intersection definition is for nef divisors; extensions to general pseudoeffective divisors require a specified definition using positive products or asymptotic growth.

For $\mathcal O(1,0)$ on $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$, $\nu=\kappa=1$ but volume is zero. On an elliptic curve every degree-zero line bundle has $\nu=0$ and volume zero. A torsion one has $\kappa=0$, whereas a nontorsion one has $\kappa=-\infty$: a degree-zero line bundle has a nonzero section only when trivial. Thus Iitaka dimension need not be a numerical invariant.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-birational-invariants-blowup" tags="application"}
Which familiar invariants of smooth complex projective surfaces survive birational maps? What changes under a point blowup?
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-birational-invariants-blowup"}
The spaces of global forms and pluricanonical sections are birationally invariant. Thus $q$, $p_g$, $\chi(\mathcal O)=1-q+p_g$, all $P_m=h^0(mK)$, and $\kappa$ are birational invariants.

For $\pi:\widetilde S\to S$ blowing up one point, the exceptional curve adds one algebraic class of square $-1$:
$$b_2(\widetilde S)=b_2(S)+1,\quad \rho(\widetilde S)=\rho(S)+1,\quad
K_{\widetilde S}^2=K_S^2-1,\quad e(\widetilde S)=e(S)+1.$$
The last two changes cancel in Noether's formula $12\chi(\mathcal O)=K^2+e$. A degree also requires a chosen polarization, so it is not an invariant of an unpolarized birational class.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-blowup-base-ideal-map" tags="application"}
Let $V=\langle s_0,\ldots,s_r\rangle\subset H^0(X,L)$ on an integral projective variety. How does its base ideal resolve the rational map $[s_0:\cdots:s_r]$? Identify the line bundle defining the resolved map and the relation with the graph.
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-blowup-base-ideal-map"}
Define $\mathcal I$ by $\operatorname{im}(V\otimes L^{-1}\to\mathcal O_X)=\mathcal I$. On $\pi:\widetilde X=\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal I}X\to X$,
$$\mathcal I\mathcal O_{\widetilde X}=\mathcal O_{\widetilde X}(-E),\qquad M=\pi^*L\otimes\mathcal O_{\widetilde X}(-E).$$
The pulled-back sections, with their common exceptional factor removed, generate $M$ and give $\widetilde X\to\mathbb P^r$. The combined map to $X\times\mathbb P^r$ identifies $\widetilde X$ with the scheme-theoretic closure of the graph. The blowup resolves the rational map but need not be smooth. A fixed Cartier divisor can first be removed from the system without changing the rational map.

Reference: [Stacks Project, blowing up](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01OF).
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-infinitely-near-exceptional-basis" tags="application"}
Blow up a smooth surface at $p$, then blow up a point $q$ on the exceptional curve. On the final surface, let $E_1$ be the total transform of the first exceptional curve and $E_2$ the second exceptional curve. Compute the strict transform of the first curve, the intersection matrix in both bases, and the relative canonical divisor.
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-infinitely-near-exceptional-basis"}
The first strict transform is $E_1'=E_1-E_2$. The total-transform basis has
$$E_1^2=E_2^2=-1,\qquad E_1E_2=0.$$
Thus $(E_1')^2=-2$, $E_1'E_2=1$, and the matrix in the irreducible exceptional basis is
$$\begin{pmatrix}-2&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}.$$
For the composite $\pi$,
$$K_{\widetilde S}=\pi^*K_S+E_1+E_2=\pi^*K_S+E_1'+2E_2.$$
The coefficient of $E_2$ depends on whether the other symbol denotes a total transform or an irreducible curve.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-infinitely-near-pencil-resolution" tags="application"}
Resolve $(x,y)\mapsto[x^2:y]$ as a rational map from $\mathbb A^2_{\mathbb C}$ to $\mathbb P^1$. Does blowing up the origin once suffice? Which exceptional curve maps nonconstantly to $\mathbb P^1$?
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-infinitely-near-pencil-resolution"}
In the chart $y=xv$, remove the common factor $x$ to get $[x:v]$, still undefined at $x=v=0$. The other first-blowup chart $x=yu$ gives $[yu^2:1]$ and has no base point.

Blow up the remaining point: the charts $v=xw$ and $x=vu$ give $[1:w]$ and $[u:1]$. The second exceptional curve maps isomorphically to $\mathbb P^1$. The strict transform of the first exceptional curve maps to $[0:1]$. The original base scheme $(x^2,y)$ has length two at one point, and its second base point is infinitely near.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-nonreduced-center-blowup" tags="application"}
Compute the blowup of $(x^2,y)\subset\mathbb C[x,y]$. Is it the ordinary blowup of the origin? Does it resolve $[x^2:y]$, and is its source smooth?
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-nonreduced-center-blowup"}
With $[U:V]=[x^2:y]$, its equation is $x^2V=yU$. On $U\ne0$, put $v=V/U$: the chart is $y=x^2v$, hence smooth with coordinates $(x,v)$. On $V\ne0$, put $u=U/V$: the chart is
$$\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C[x,y,u]/(x^2-yu),$$
with an $A_1$ singularity at the origin. Projection to $[U:V]$ resolves the rational map. Unlike the smooth ordinary point blowup, this blowup is singular: the scheme structure of the center matters. Successive ordinary blowups at the origin and the remaining infinitely near base point give a smooth resolution of the map.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-hirzebruch-elementary-transform" tags="application"}
Let $S\subset\mathbb F_n$ be the section with $S^2=-n$, $n\ge1$. Blow up a point $p$ and contract the strict transform of its ruling fiber. Which Hirzebruch surface results when $p\in S$ and when $p\notin S$?
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-hirzebruch-elementary-transform"}
The fiber through $p$ acquires square $-1$, so it can be contracted to a smooth point. If $p\in S$, the strict transform of $S$ has square $-n-1$ and is disjoint from the contracted fiber; the result is $\mathbb F_{n+1}$.

If $p\notin S$, the section still has square $-n$ and meets the contracted fiber once. Contraction raises its square by one, giving $\mathbb F_{n-1}$. Starting from $\mathbb F_0$, either choice gives $\mathbb F_1$. These elementary transformations give explicit birational maps among ruled surfaces while preserving their ruling over $\mathbb P^1$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-cremona-curve-transform" tags="application"}
A plane curve of degree $d$ has multiplicities $m_1,m_2,m_3$ at the coordinate points and contains no coordinate line. Under the quadratic Cremona transformation, compute its image degree and multiplicities at the inverse base points. Apply this to a conic through all three points.
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-cremona-curve-transform"}
On the common blowup its class is $D=dH-\sum m_iE_i$, while a target line pulls back to $H'=2H-E_1-E_2-E_3$. Thus
$$d'=DH'=2d-m_1-m_2-m_3,\qquad m_i'=d-m_j-m_k\quad(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}).$$
The second formula intersects $D$ with the contracted line $H-E_j-E_k$. A conic through the three points becomes a line avoiding the inverse base points: $d'=1$ and all $m_i'=0$. The exclusion of coordinate components prevents a contracted component from invalidating this interpretation.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-mori-blowup-plane" tags="application"}
Let $X=\operatorname{Bl}_p\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb C}$, with line pullback $H$ and exceptional curve $E$. Determine $\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)$, the canonical degree of each extremal generator, and both contractions. When is $D=aH-bE$ nef or ample?
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-mori-blowup-plane"}
Put $f=H-E$. A nonexceptional integral curve has class $dH-mE=d f+(d-m)E$ with $0\le m\le d$, so
$$\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)=\mathbb R_{\ge0}[E]+\mathbb R_{\ge0}[f].$$
Since $K_X=-3H+E$, $K_XE=-1$ and $K_Xf=-2$. The first ray contracts by the blowdown to $\mathbb P^2$; the second by the pencil of lines through $p$ to $\mathbb P^1$. The blowdown is divisorial and the pencil is of fiber type. Since $DE=b$ and $Df=a-b$, nefness means $a\ge b\ge0$, and ampleness means $a>b>0$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-mori-hirzebruch-sign" tags="application"}
On the complex Hirzebruch surface $\mathbb F_n$ with $n\ge1$, let $S^2=-n$ be the negative section and $f$ a fiber. Identify the curve cone and decide which extremal rays the $K$-negative contraction theorem covers. Can the negative section be contracted when the theorem does not cover it?
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-mori-hirzebruch-sign"}
One has
$$\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(\mathbb F_n)=\mathbb R_{\ge0}[S]+\mathbb R_{\ge0}[f],\qquad K=-2S-(n+2)f,$$
so $Kf=-2$ and $KS=n-2$. The fiber ray always gives the ruling. The section ray is $K$-negative only for $n=1$, when it gives the blowdown to $\mathbb P^2$. It is $K$-trivial for $n=2$ and $K$-positive for $n>2$. Nevertheless, $|S+nf|$ contracts $S$ to the vertex of the cone over the degree-$n$ rational normal curve. For $n\ge2$ the target is singular. Negative self-intersection alone does not imply $K$-negativity.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-mori-finiteness" tags="application"}
For a complex projective klt pair $(X,\Delta)$, what finite reduction does the cone theorem give after fixing an ample divisor $A$ and $\varepsilon>0$? What stronger conclusion follows if $-(K_X+\Delta)$ is ample?
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-mori-finiteness"}
Only finitely many extremal rays are negative for $K_X+\Delta+\varepsilon A$, and
$$\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)=\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)_{K_X+\Delta+\varepsilon A\ge0}
+\sum_{j=1}^r\mathbb R_{\ge0}[C_j].$$
The finite set can grow as $\varepsilon$ tends to zero. If $-(K_X+\Delta)$ is ample, take $A=-(K_X+\Delta)$ and $0<\varepsilon<1$. The nonnegative part is then $\{0\}$, so the whole curve cone is rational polyhedral. Nefness of a divisor can consequently be checked on finitely many rational curve generators.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-mori-k3-boundary" tags="application"}
Let $X$ be a complex projective K3 surface containing a smooth rational curve $C$. Does the cone theorem assert that $[C]$ is a $K_X$-negative ray? Does it imply that the whole curve cone is rational polyhedral? Can $C$ still be contracted?
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-mori-k3-boundary"}
Since $K_X=0$, every curve class is $K_X$-trivial, so the $K_X$-negative part is empty. The cone theorem gives no rational-polyhedral conclusion for the whole cone. Adjunction gives $C^2=-2$. For an ample Cartier divisor $A$, take
$$D=2A+(A\cdot C)C.$$
Then $DC=0$, $D^2=4A^2+2(A\cdot C)^2>0$, and $D$ intersects every other integral curve positively. Base-point-freeness for nef and big divisors on a K3 makes $D$ semiample. Its morphism contracts precisely $C$ to an $A_1$ singularity. This is a $K_X$-trivial contraction.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-mori-length-degree" tags="application"}
Let $X$ be a smooth complex Fano $n$-fold with $-K_X=rH$, where $H$ is ample Cartier and $r>0$ is an integer. What bound on $H$-degree can you use when searching for rational generators of extremal rays? Specialize to $n=3$, $r=2$.
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-mori-length-degree"}
Every extremal ray has a rational curve generator $C$ with $0<-K_XC\le n+1$. Hence
$$1\le H\cdot C\le\left\lfloor\frac{n+1}{r}\right\rfloor.$$
For an index-two Fano threefold, it suffices to find a generator of $H$-degree one or two on each ray. The bound controls a choice of generator, not the degrees of all curves in the ray.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-mori-relative-cone" tags="application"}
Let $\pi:X=\operatorname{Bl}_p\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb C}\to\mathbb P^2$, with classes $H,E$. Determine $\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/\mathbb P^2)$. For $D=aH-bE$, compare relative nefness with absolute nefness. Can the ruling $X\to\mathbb P^1$ be performed over this fixed base?
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-mori-relative-cone"}
The relative cone records curves contracted by $\pi$, so
$$\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X/\mathbb P^2)=\mathbb R_{\ge0}[E].$$
Relative nefness requires $DE=b\ge0$; absolute nefness also requires $D(H-E)=a-b\ge0$. The ruling contracts curves mapping to lines in $\mathbb P^2$, so it is not a contraction over $\mathbb P^2$. Relative Mori theory only contracts vertical curve classes.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-mori-nef-threshold" tags="application"}
On $X=\operatorname{Bl}_p\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb C}$, set $A=2H-E$. Compute the nef threshold
$$\lambda=\inf\{t\ge0:K_X+tA\text{ is nef}\}.$$
Which ray is orthogonal to $K_X+\lambda A$, and what contraction does it determine?
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-mori-nef-threshold"}
The cone generators are $E$ and $f=H-E$, with $AE=Af=1$, $K_XE=-1$, and $K_Xf=-2$. Thus nefness requires $t\ge1$ and $t\ge2$, giving $\lambda=2$. At the threshold,
$$K_X+2A=H-E=f,\qquad fE=1,\quad f^2=0.$$
The orthogonal ray is $\mathbb R_{\ge0}[f]$, and $|H-E|$ gives the ruling to $\mathbb P^1$. This is the ray selected by this choice of scaling divisor.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-mori-contraction-types" tags="application"}
Let $(X,\Delta)$ be a complex projective $\mathbb Q$-factorial klt pair, and let $f:X\to Y$ contract a $(K_X+\Delta)$-negative extremal ray. Which geometric tests distinguish the possible next steps in the minimal model program? What extra construction is needed in the small case?
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-mori-contraction-types"}
If $\dim Y<\dim X$, the contraction is of fiber type and gives a log Mori fiber space. If $f$ is birational and contracts a divisor, it is divisorial; continue on $Y$ with the pushed-forward boundary. If $f$ is birational with exceptional locus of codimension at least two, it is small. Then the next step is the flip $X\dashrightarrow X^+$ over $Y$, with $K_{X^+}+\Delta^+$ ample over $Y$. The contraction theorem supplies $f$, not the flip. For a ray contraction, $\rho(X/Y)=1$.
:::


::: {.front card-id="card-21139fdf3f221644"}
What is **Kodaira dimension**?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-21139fdf3f221644"}
For $X$ normal integral projective and $D$ a $\mathbb Q$-Cartier divisor, measure section growth by
$$\kappa(X,D)=\limsup_{\substack{m\to\infty\\mD\ {\rm Cartier}\\h^0(mD)>0}}\frac{\log h^0(X,\mathcal O_X(mD))}{\log m},$$
with value $-\infty$ if no positive Cartier multiple has a nonzero section. For smooth projective $X$, the Kodaira dimension is the Iitaka dimension of the canonical bundle:
$$\kappa(X)=\kappa(X;\omega_X).$$
Concretely, $\kappa(X)=-\infty$ if $P_m(X)=0$ for all $m>0$; otherwise it is the smallest integer $k$ for which $P_m(X)=O(m^k)$. Interpret $P_m(X)\asymp m^{\kappa(X)}$ as upper and lower bounds by positive constant multiples for sufficiently large, sufficiently divisible $m$. Thus $\kappa(X)\in\{-\infty,0,1,\ldots,\dim X\}$. For a singular complex projective variety, its birational Kodaira dimension is computed on a smooth projective resolution; using reflexive canonical powers on the singular model needs additional hypotheses.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-0996fd4a0ee4d958"}
What is a variety of general type?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-0996fd4a0ee4d958"}
Maximal possible Kodaira dimension: $\kappa(X) = \dim(X)$, i.e. $\omega_X$ is big. E.g. any variety with ample canonical.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-67f625f64dc3c847"}
What is a big line bundle (big divisor)?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-67f625f64dc3c847"}
$\mcl\in \operatorname{Pic}(X)$ is big iff $\kappa(X; \mcl) = \dim(X)$ is maximal, i.e. $\dim \operatorname{im}\phi_{{\left\lvert {m\mcl} \right\rvert}} = \dim(X)$ for some $m\gg 0$.

Equivalently, sections grow maximally: there is $c > 0$ with $h^0({\mathcal{O}}(mD)) \geq c\cdot m^{\dim X}$ for all sufficiently large and divisible $m$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-a54e2a90c7831a3d"}
What is Iitaka dimension?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-a54e2a90c7831a3d"}
For a line bundle $\mcl$ on an integral projective variety $X$, define its *Iitaka dimension* as the maximal image dimension of its nonempty positive-power systems:
$$\kappa(X;\mcl)=\max_{\substack{m>0\\H^0(X,\mcl^{\otimes m})\ne0}}\dim\overline{\phi_{|m\mcl|}(X)},\qquad
\phi_{|m\mcl|}:X\dashrightarrow\mathbb P(H^0(X,\mcl^{\otimes m})^\vee).$$
These are rational maps, not necessarily embeddings or morphisms. Always $\kappa(X;\mcl)\in\{-\infty,0,1,\ldots,\dim X\}$.

If every positive power of $\mcl$ has no section, define $\kappa(X;\mcl)=-\infty$. Non-effectivity of $\mcl$ alone does not suffice: on a complex Enriques surface $H^0(\omega_X)=0$ but $\omega_X^{\otimes2}\cong\mathcal O_X$, so $\kappa(X;\omega_X)=0$.

When a positive power has a section, the equivalent section-ring formula is
$$\kappa(X; \mcl) + 1 = \trdeg_k \operatorname{ff}(R(X; L))$$
where $L=\mcl$ and $R(X;L)$ is the section ring.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-e9e007319ee4649c"}
What is the section ring?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-e9e007319ee4649c"}
$$R(X, L) \coloneqq\bigoplus_{n\geq 0} H^0(X; \mcl{ {}^{ \scriptstyle\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_X}^{n} }  })$$
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-e5cda52b3ce32ece"}
Classify curves by Kodaira dimension.
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-e5cda52b3ce32ece"}
$\kappa(X) = -\infty \iff g=0$, positive curvature.

$\implies X\cong {\mathbf{P}}^1 \implies P_{\gt 0}(X) = 0$.

$\kappa(X) = 0 \iff g=1$, flat.

$\implies X\cong E$ an elliptic curve

$\implies K_X$ trivial.

$\kappa(X) = 1 \iff g\geq 2$, negative curvature.

$\implies$ general type.

$\implies K_X$ ample.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-b361751d37669bf3" tags="theorem"}
Classify smooth minimal complex projective surfaces by Kodaira dimension.
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-b361751d37669bf3"}
Define $\kappa(X) \coloneqq\operatorname{trdeg}_k \left( \bigoplus_{n\geq 0} H^0({\mathcal{O}}_X(nK_X)) \right) - 1$, or $-\infty$ if all $H^0(nK_X) = 0$ for $n > 0$. For a minimal smooth complex projective surface $X$,

$\kappa(X) = -\infty \iff$Ruled, rational.

$\implies X\cong {\mathbf{P}}^{2}$ or a minimal ruled surface.

$\implies \left|12 K_{X}\right|=\emptyset$.

$\kappa(X)=0$ if and only if $X$ is abelian, bielliptic (also called hyperelliptic), K3, or Enriques.

$\implies \left|12 K_{X}\right|=\{0\}$.

$\kappa(X)=1$ implies that $X$ is elliptic; these are the properly elliptic surfaces.

$\kappa(X) = 2 \iff$ general type.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-edd9ac6f0fa2d3e8"}
What are the **plurigenera** of a smooth projective variety?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-edd9ac6f0fa2d3e8"}
$$P_m(X)= h^0(\omega_X^m) = h^0(\mathcal{O}_X(m K_X)) = h^0( (\operatorname{det}\Omega_{X/k})^{\otimes m} )$$
This is a birational invariant. Measures the size of a canonical model of $X$, $\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}(\bigoplus_{m\geq 0} H^0(K_X^m))$.

Can prove a variety is not rational by showing $P_m(X) > 0$ for some $m>0$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-cd129aadf37058b3"}
What is the **irregularity** of a smooth projective variety?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-cd129aadf37058b3"}
The Hodge number
$$q(X) \coloneqq h^{0, 1} = h^1({\mathcal{O}}_X)$$
For a smooth complex projective variety, $q(X)=h^0(\Omega_X^1)$ by Hodge symmetry.

For a surface, it measures the difference $q = p_g(X) - p_a(X)$ between geometric and arithmetic genus.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-66ce9a474b7f4659"}
What is a **rational** variety?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-66ce9a474b7f4659"}
Normal and birational $X\overset{\sim}{\dashrightarrow}{\mathbf{P}}^N$ for some $N$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-887ac50e6202d5f9"}
What is a **unirational** variety?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-887ac50e6202d5f9"}
Idea: Luroth shows that if $k \subsetneq L \subseteq k(x)$ then $L = k(x)$ and asks the same for subfields $L \subseteq k(x_1,\cdots, x_n)$. If $L = k(X)$ for a variety, this induces ${\mathbf{P}}^n \dashrightarrow X$ with dense image; call such $X$ unirational.

Normal and admits a dominant rational map ${\mathbf{P}}^N\dashrightarrow X$ for some $N$, or dominated by a rational variety so that $k({\mathbf{P}}^n)/k(X)$ is a purely transcendental (separable) extension of finite type.

Idea: similar to rational, e.g. $h^0(\Omega^{\geq 1}_X) = 0$, Luroth asks if unirational implies rational. True for curves, false for 3-folds and higher.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-c99c177d102ccfce"}
What is a **uniruled** variety?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-c99c177d102ccfce"}
Idea: covered by rational curves, for every $x\in X$ there is a nonconstant morphism ${\mathbf{P}}^1\to X$ with $f(0) =x$, i.e. for every point one can find a rational curve containing it.

More precisely, $\exists Y$ and a dominant rational morphism $Y\times {\mathbf{P}}^1\dashrightarrow X$ which does not factor through the projection to $Y$, i.e. dominated by a ruled variety.

::: {.front card-id="card-9db7a801cc266d51"}
What is the **blowup** of a variety?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-9db7a801cc266d51"}
For $\operatorname{Bl}_0 {\mathbf{A}}^n$: take ${\mathbf{A}}^n\times {\mathbf{P}}^{n-1}$ with coordinates $(x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1})$, $[y_0:\cdots:y_{n-1}]$. Closed subschemes are cut out by equations polynomial in the $x_i,y_i$ and homogeneous in the $y_i$. Take the blowup to be
$$\{x_iy_j=x_jy_i\mid0\le i<j\le n-1\},$$
the common zero locus of the $2\times2$ minors of
$$\begin{bmatrix}x_0&x_1&\cdots&x_{n-1}\\y_0&y_1&\cdots&y_{n-1}\end{bmatrix}.$$
Then $\exists \pi: \operatorname{Bl}_0 {\mathbf{A}}^n \to {\mathbf{A}}^n$ where ${\sharp}\pi^{-1}(p) = 1$ for $p\neq 0$ and $\pi^{-1}(0)\cong {\mathbf{P}}^{n-1}$ corresponding to all lines through $0$.

For a positive-dimensional integral closed subvariety $Y\subseteq{\mathbf{A}}^n$ containing $0$, take $\cl_{\operatorname{Bl}_0 {\mathbf{A}}^n}\qty{\pi^{-1}(Y\setminus\left\{{0}\right\})}$. This is the strict transform of $Y$, isomorphic to $\operatorname{Bl}_0Y$. To blow up the ambient ${\mathbf{A}}^n$ along $Y$ instead, use the Rees algebra of the ideal of $Y$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-77f1ef48e07e3dc0"}
What is the **blowup of a scheme along a subscheme**? How does this recover blowups for varieties?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-77f1ef48e07e3dc0"}
Can generally blowup along a coherent sheaf of ideals $\mci$ on $X$: take the sheaf of graded algebras $\tilde \mci\coloneqq\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}\mci^d$ where $\mci^0 \coloneqq{\mathcal{O}}_X$ and set $\tilde X \coloneqq\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}\tilde \mci$. Can do this with the ideal of sheaf of $Y\hookrightarrow X$ a closed subscheme.

For $X={\mathbf{A}}^n_k=\operatorname{Spec}A$, set $A=k[x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1}]$ and $I=(x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1})$, the ideal of $p=0$. Write the Rees algebra as $S=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}I^dt^d\subset A[t]$. There is a graded surjection
$$A[y_0,\ldots,y_{n-1}]\twoheadrightarrow S,\qquad y_i\mapsto x_it,$$
where elements of $A$ have degree zero and the $y_i$ and $x_it$ have degree one. Thus $\tilde X=\operatorname{Proj}S$ is a closed subscheme of $\operatorname{Proj}A[y_0,\ldots,y_{n-1}]\cong\mathbb P^{n-1}_A$, with ideal generated by $x_iy_j-x_jy_i$. This recovers the incidence equations.

::: {.front card-id="application-extremal-product-contractions" tags="application"}
For $S=\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb C}\times\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb C}$, identify the extremal rays of the curve cone and their contractions. Are these contractions birational?
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-extremal-product-contractions"}
The cone is generated by the two fiber classes, since effective curve classes have nonnegative bidegrees. Each fiber has $K_S$-degree $-2$, so each ray is $K_S$-negative. The associated contraction is the projection that collapses those fibers. Both contractions have one-dimensional target and are of fiber type, not birational.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-fano-nef-semiample" tags="application"}
Let $X$ be smooth complex projective with $-K_X$ ample, and let $D$ be nef Cartier. Must some positive multiple of $D$ be globally generated? Choose a theorem and check its positivity assumption.
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-fano-nef-semiample"}
Yes. The divisor $D-K_X$ is ample, being the sum of a nef divisor and an ample divisor, so it is nef and big. The base-point-free theorem applies with boundary zero and $a=1$, giving base-point-freeness of $|mD|$ for every sufficiently large $m$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-resolve-plane-pencil" tags="application"}
Resolve the rational map $\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb C}\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb C}$, $[x:y:z]\mapsto[x:y]$. Give its indeterminacy locus, a smooth resolution, and the fibers of the resulting morphism.
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-resolve-plane-pencil"}
The only indeterminacy point is $p=[0:0:1]$. Blowing up $p$ resolves the pencil of lines through $p$. The resulting morphism $\operatorname{Bl}_p\mathbb P^2\to\mathbb P^1$ has fibers the strict transforms of those lines, each isomorphic to $\mathbb P^1$; the exceptional curve records the direction and is a section.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-iitaka-elliptic-fibration" tags="application"}
Let $S\to B$ be a surjective morphism with connected fibers from a smooth complex projective surface to a smooth projective curve, with general fiber elliptic. Can $S$ be of general type? Which theorem gives a bound without computing $K_S^2$?
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-iitaka-elliptic-fibration"}
No. The general fiber has Kodaira dimension zero. Iitaka's easy addition theorem gives $\kappa(S)\le0+\dim B=1$, whereas a surface of general type has Kodaira dimension two.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-iitaka-rational-fibration" tags="application"}
Let $X\to Y$ be a surjective morphism of smooth complex projective varieties with connected fibers and general fiber $\mathbb P^1$. Can $X$ have a nonzero pluricanonical section? Choose the relevant form of the addition theorem.
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-iitaka-rational-fibration"}
No. The general fiber has Kodaira dimension $-\infty$, so Iitaka's easy addition theorem gives $\kappa(X)=-\infty$. Thus $H^0(X,mK_X)=0$ for every $m>0$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-canonical-model-general-type" tags="application"}
Let $X$ be a smooth complex projective $n$-fold of general type. Which results give a projective canonical model and show that its dimension is $n$?
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-canonical-model-general-type"}
BCHM gives finite generation of $R(X,K_X)$, hence a projective model $\operatorname{Proj}R(X,K_X)$. General type means $\kappa(X)=n$; Iitaka's section-growth theorem gives $h^0(X,mK_X)\asymp m^n$ for sufficiently divisible $m$. Consequently the canonical model has dimension $n$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-rationality-enriques-test" tags="application"}
A smooth complex projective surface has $q=p_g=0$. Is that enough to conclude rationality? Give a counterexample and identify the extra plurigenus test that does decide rationality.
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-rationality-enriques-test"}
An Enriques surface has $q=p_g=0$ but $P_2=1$, and is not rational. Castelnuovo's criterion uses $q=0$ and $P_2=0$, not merely $P_1=p_g=0$. Under these two vanishing conditions the surface is rational.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-minimal-model-uniqueness-boundary" tags="application"}
Compare minimal-model uniqueness for a smooth complex projective surface with $\kappa\ge0$ and for a rational surface. Why do $\mathbb F_0$ and $\mathbb F_2$ test the hypothesis?
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-minimal-model-uniqueness-boundary"}
A surface with $\kappa\ge0$ has a unique smooth minimal model up to isomorphism. Rational surfaces need not: $\mathbb F_0$ and $\mathbb F_2$ are birational and minimal, but are not isomorphic. The latter has a curve of self-intersection $-2$, whereas all effective curve classes on $\mathbb F_0=\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$ have nonnegative square.
:::

::: {.front card-id="theorem-indeterminacy-codimension" tags="theorem"}
For a rational map $f:X\dashrightarrow Y$ with $X$ a normal integral variety and $Y$ proper over the base field, bound the codimension of its indeterminacy locus. What follows for a surface?
:::

::: {.back card-id="theorem-indeterminacy-codimension"}
The map is defined at every codimension-one point, so its indeterminacy locus has codimension at least two. On a surface it is a finite set of closed points. At a codimension-one point, normality gives a discrete valuation ring, and properness extends the map by the valuative criterion.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-rational-map-curve-extension" tags="application"}
Compare rational maps from a smooth projective curve to a proper variety with the rational function $1/t:\mathbb P^1\dashrightarrow\mathbb A^1$. Which maps extend everywhere, and which hypothesis decides?
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-rational-map-curve-extension"}
Every rational map from a smooth projective curve to a proper variety extends uniquely: a curve has no nonempty closed subset of codimension at least two. The function $1/t$ does not extend as a morphism to $\mathbb A^1$ at $t=0$. Its target is not proper; regarded as a map to $\mathbb P^1$, it extends by taking the value infinity there.
:::

::: {.front card-id="theorem-miyaoka-mori-bend-break" tags="theorem"}
Let $X$ be a smooth projective $n$-fold over an algebraically closed field, $C\subset X$ an integral curve with $K_X\cdot C<0$, and $H$ a nef Cartier divisor. State the classical Miyaoka–Mori bend-and-break conclusion through a closed point $x\in C$, with its $H$-degree bound.
:::

::: {.back card-id="theorem-miyaoka-mori-bend-break"}
There is an integral rational curve $R\subset X$ through $x$ with
$$H\cdot R\le 2n\,\frac{H\cdot C}{-K_X\cdot C}.$$
See Miyaoka–Mori, Theorem 5, as recalled in the [introduction to Optimal bounds in Bend-and-Break](https://doi.org/10.1017/fmp.2026.10033).
:::

::: {.front card-id="theorem-deformations-maps-curves" tags="theorem"}
Let $C$ be a smooth projective curve over an algebraically closed field, $X$ a smooth quasi-projective variety, and $f:C\to X$. Identify the tangent and obstruction spaces for deforming $f$ with $C$ fixed. Give a sufficient condition for smoothness of $\operatorname{Hom}(C,X)$ at $[f]$.
:::

::: {.back card-id="theorem-deformations-maps-curves"}
The tangent space is $H^0(C,f^*T_X)$, and obstructions lie in $H^1(C,f^*T_X)$. If the latter vanishes, $\operatorname{Hom}(C,X)$ is smooth at $[f]$, of dimension $h^0(C,f^*T_X)$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="theorem-free-rational-curve-evaluation" tags="theorem"}
For a nonconstant map $f:\mathbb P^1\to X$ to a smooth quasi-projective variety over an algebraically closed field, write $f^*T_X=\bigoplus_i\mathcal O(a_i)$. What condition makes $f$ free, and what does it imply for deformations and evaluation?
:::

::: {.back card-id="theorem-free-rational-curve-evaluation"}
The map is free if every $a_i\ge0$. Then $H^1(f^*T_X)=0$ and $H^1(f^*T_X(-p))=0$ for each $p\in\mathbb P^1$. The Hom scheme is smooth at $[f]$, and evaluation $\operatorname{ev}_p:\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb P^1,X)\to X$ is smooth there. The universal evaluation $\mathbb P^1\times\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb P^1,X)\to X$ is smooth at $(p,[f])$ as well.
:::

::: {.front card-id="application-unobstructed-not-free-map" tags="application"}
Suppose $f:\mathbb P^1\to X$ has $f^*T_X\simeq\mathcal O(2)\oplus\mathcal O(-1)$. Is the Hom scheme smooth at $[f]$? Is $f$ free? Is evaluation at a fixed point smooth there?
:::

::: {.back card-id="application-unobstructed-not-free-map"}
$H^1(f^*T_X)=0$, so the Hom scheme is smooth at $[f]$. The negative summand makes $f$ nonfree. Evaluation is not smooth: its tangent map $H^0(f^*T_X)\to(f^*T_X)_p$ misses the $\mathcal O(-1)$ direction, since that summand has no sections.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-acf2c9c2a6e37c9a" tags="definition"}
What is the definition of (-1)-curve?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-acf2c9c2a6e37c9a"}
A $(-1)$-curve on a smooth projective surface is a smooth rational curve $E\cong\mathbb P^1$ with $E^2=-1$. By Castelnuovo's criterion it can be contracted to a smooth point.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-f5c95b91658d022a" tags="definition"}
What is the definition of Abelian variety?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-f5c95b91658d022a"}
An abelian variety over a field $k$ is a complete connected group variety over $k$; over $\mathbb C$ it is equivalently a complex torus $\mathbb C^g/\Lambda$ admitting a projective embedding. Its group law is commutative and algebraic.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-8a18ef59d8abdf94" tags="definition"}
What is the definition of Constant scalar curvature kähler metric?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-8a18ef59d8abdf94"}
A Kähler metric $\omega$ on a complex manifold is a constant scalar curvature Kähler (cscK) metric if its scalar curvature $S(\omega)=g^{i\bar j}R_{i\bar j}$ is a constant function.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-86fb2447b24e41dd" tags="definition"}
What is the definition of Different?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-86fb2447b24e41dd"}
For a log pair $(X,S+\Delta)$ with $S$ a reduced divisor and $K_X+S+\Delta$ $\mathbb Q$-Cartier, the different $\mathrm{Diff}_{S^\nu}(\Delta)$ is the effective $\mathbb Q$-divisor on the normalization $S^\nu$ defined by $\nu^*(K_X+S+\Delta)|_S=K_{S^\nu}+\mathrm{Diff}_{S^\nu}(\Delta)$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-814740f33155ce31" tags="definition"}
What is the definition of Exceptional locus?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-814740f33155ce31"}
For a birational morphism $f:X\to Y$, the exceptional locus is the closed subset $E\subset X$ on which $f$ is not an isomorphism onto its image; equivalently it is the complement of the largest open set over which $f$ is an isomorphism. When $E$ is a divisor it is the exceptional divisor.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-b2b094296f5c90a6" tags="definition"}
What is the definition of Extremal contraction?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-b2b094296f5c90a6"}
An extremal contraction is the projective morphism $\varphi_F:X\to Y$ with connected fibers associated to a $(K_X+\Delta)$-negative extremal face $F$ of $\overline{NE}(X)$; it is characterized by $\varphi_F(C)$ being a point if and only if $[C]\in F$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-fd07ea94087ee7e4" tags="definition"}
What is the definition of F-exceptional?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-fd07ea94087ee7e4"}
For a morphism $f:Y\to X$, a divisor $E\subset Y$ is $f$-exceptional if $\operatorname{codim}_X f(E)\ge2$; a divisor is $f$-exceptional when each of its irreducible components is.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-4c4388e6f992f9fa" tags="definition"}
What is the definition of Fiber-type contraction?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-4c4388e6f992f9fa"}
A projective surjective morphism $f:X\to Y$ with connected fibers is of fiber type if $\dim Y<\dim X$. For a projective $\mathbb Q$-factorial klt pair $(X,\Delta)$, a log Mori fiber space additionally requires
$$\rho(X/Y)=1,\qquad -(K_X+\Delta)\text{ is }f\text{-ample}.$$
For $\Delta=0$ this is a Mori fiber space in the klt convention. A contraction of a higher-dimensional face can have relative Picard number greater than one.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-859e3263b428349d" tags="definition"}
What is the definition of Free rational curve?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-859e3263b428349d"}
A nonconstant map $f:\mathbb P^1\to X$ is a free rational curve if $f^*T_X\cong\bigoplus_i\mathcal O(a_i)$ with every $a_i\ge0$; equivalently $H^1(\mathbb P^1,f^*T_X)=0$. Then deformations of $f$ are unobstructed and evaluation maps are smooth.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-a938bcb551387242" tags="definition"}
What is the definition of Geometric invariant theory?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-a938bcb551387242"}
Geometric invariant theory (GIT) constructs quotients of a variety $X$ by a reductive group $G$ as $X^{ss}/\!/G=\operatorname{Proj}\bigoplus_{m\ge0}H^0(X,\mathcal L^{\otimes m})^G$, using the semistable locus and a $G$-linearized ample line bundle $\mathcal L$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-715171414ed7f3f9" tags="definition"}
What is the definition of Iitaka fibration?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-715171414ed7f3f9"}
For a smooth projective variety $X$ with $\kappa(X)\ge0$, the Iitaka fibration is the rational map $\varphi_{|mK_X|}:X\dashrightarrow Y$ for $m\gg0$ sufficiently divisible. It is birationally independent of $m$, has $\dim Y=\kappa(X)$, and its general fiber has Kodaira dimension zero.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-db8009b44f3c6551" tags="definition"}
What is the definition of Numerical equivalence?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-db8009b44f3c6551"}
Two divisor classes $D_1,D_2\in N^1(X)_{\mathbb R}$ are numerically equivalent if $D_1\cdot C=D_2\cdot C$ for every irreducible curve $C\subset X$; two curve classes are numerically equivalent if they have the same intersection number with every divisor. The numerical class of a divisor is its image in $N^1(X)_{\mathbb R}=(\mathrm{Div}(X)/\equiv)\otimes\mathbb R$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-9972162b653b2027" tags="definition"}
What is the definition of Plt?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-9972162b653b2027"}
A log pair $(X,\Delta)$ is purely log terminal (plt) if every exceptional divisor $E$ over $X$ has discrepancy $a(E;X,\Delta)>-1$, and every $E$ with $a(E;X,\Delta)\le 0$ has center contained in the reduced boundary $\lfloor\Delta\rfloor$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-987c64ec26849bc7" tags="definition"}
What is the definition of Q-cartier?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-987c64ec26849bc7"}
A $\mathbb Q$-Weil divisor $D$ on a normal variety is $\mathbb Q$-Cartier if $mD$ is Cartier for some integer $m>0$; equivalently $D$ lies in $\mathrm{CDiv}(X)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-5c72b9ad61a841b4" tags="definition"}
What is the definition of Scheme-theoretic closure?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-5c72b9ad61a841b4"}
For a locally closed subscheme $Z\subset X$ contained in an open subscheme $U\subset X$, the scheme-theoretic closure of $Z$ in $X$ is the closed subscheme defined by $\ker(\mathcal O_X\to j_*\mathcal O_Z)$, where $j:U\hookrightarrow X$ is the inclusion; it is the smallest closed subscheme of $X$ containing $Z$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-5b19b21c81614d35" tags="definition"}
What is the definition of Semiample?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-5b19b21c81614d35"}
A line bundle $\mathcal L$ on a projective variety is semiample if $\mathcal L^{\otimes m}$ is globally generated for some $m>0$; equivalently the complete linear system $|mL|$ is base-point-free and defines a morphism for some $m>0$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-338da2f604bf5b4b" tags="definition"}
What is the definition of Smooth locus?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-338da2f604bf5b4b"}
The smooth locus $X^{\mathrm{sm}}\subset X$ is the open subset of points at which $X$ is smooth (regular); its complement is the singular locus $X^{\mathrm{sing}}$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-be3216d4ed9f3f10" tags="definition"}
What is the definition of Valuative criterion?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-be3216d4ed9f3f10"}
The valuative criterion for properness: a morphism $f:X\to Y$ of finite type is proper if and only if for every DVR $R$ with fraction field $K$, every commutative square $\operatorname{Spec}K\to X$, $\operatorname{Spec}R\to Y$ admits a unique lifting $\operatorname{Spec}R\to X$. Separatedness requires only uniqueness.
:::
