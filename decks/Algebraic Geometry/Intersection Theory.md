::: {.front card-id="intersection-cycles-equivalences" tags="definition comparison"}
Define cycles and rational equivalence. Compare rational, algebraic, homological, and numerical equivalence on smooth projective complex varieties, using divisors on an elliptic curve.
:::

::: {.back card-id="intersection-cycles-equivalences"}
$Z_k(X)$ is the free abelian group on integral $k$-dimensional closed subvarieties. Rational equivalence is generated by $\operatorname{div}_W(f)$ for integral $(k+1)$-dimensional $W$ and $f\in k(W)^\times$; $\operatorname{CH}_k=Z_k/\sim_{\mathrm{rat}}$.

| Equivalence | Test for the difference |
| --- | --- |
| Rational | Sum of principal divisors on larger subvarieties |
| Algebraic | Differences of fibers of cycle families over connected smooth curves |
| Homological, with rational coefficients | Zero under the cycle map to $H^{2p}(X,\mathbb Q)$ |
| Numerical | Zero intersection with every complementary-dimensional cycle |

Rational $\Rightarrow$ algebraic $\Rightarrow$ rational homological $\Rightarrow$ numerical. On an elliptic curve, $[P]-[O]$ has degree zero, hence is algebraically, homologically, and numerically trivial. It is rationally trivial iff $P=O$, by $E\cong\operatorname{Pic}^0(E)$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="intersection-moving-product" tags="theorem construction application" cite="[@The25, tag 0B0D]"}
State the moving lemma on a smooth projective variety and explain how it permits intersection products. Does it say every effective cycle moves in an effective family?
:::

::: {.back card-id="intersection-moving-product"}
Over an algebraically closed field, a cycle is rationally equivalent to one meeting a prescribed finite collection of subvarieties properly. Proper intersection means the expected codimension, or empty intersection when that codimension exceeds the dimension.

On smooth $X$, this yields a well-defined product
$$\operatorname{CH}^p(X)\times\operatorname{CH}^q(X)\to\operatorname{CH}^{p+q}(X).$$
Move a representative, then intersect with intersection multiplicities. A replacement may have negative coefficients: the lemma does not assert movement in a basepoint-free linear system. An exceptional curve $E$ on a blown-up surface has $E^2=-1$ and is rigid; its self-intersection is still defined.
:::

::: {.front card-id="intersection-operations-projection" tags="formula comparison application"}
State proper pushforward, flat pullback, regular-embedding Gysin pullback, and the projection formula on Chow groups. Include dimension shifts and a finite-map example.
:::

::: {.back card-id="intersection-operations-projection"}
| Operation | Definition or degree |
| --- | --- |
| Proper $f_*:\operatorname{CH}_k(X)\to\operatorname{CH}_k(Y)$ | $[V]\mapsto[k(V):k(f(V))][f(V)]$ if dimensions agree; zero otherwise |
| Flat $f^*:\operatorname{CH}_k(Y)\to\operatorname{CH}_{k+r}(X)$, relative dimension $r$ | Scheme-theoretic inverse image with generic lengths |
| Regular immersion $i^!:\operatorname{CH}_k(Y)\to\operatorname{CH}_{k-c}(Z)$, codimension $c$ | Refined pullback, defined even without proper intersection |

For proper $f:X\to Y$ between smooth varieties, $f_*(f^*\alpha\cdot\beta)=\alpha\cdot f_*\beta$. If $f$ is finite flat of degree $d$, $f_*f^*\alpha=d\alpha$.

For $[n]:E\to E$ in characteristic zero, $d=n^2$; the pullback of a point is the sum of its $n^2$ preimages, not $n$ times a point.
:::

::: {.front card-id="intersection-local-multiplicity" tags="definition application"}
Compute the intersection multiplicity of $y=0$ with $y=x^m$ at the origin of $\mathbb A^2_{\mathbb C}$. When does quotient length compute intersection multiplicity, and what is the general Tor formula?
:::

::: {.back card-id="intersection-local-multiplicity"}
$$I_0=\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C[[x,y]]/(y,y-x^m)=\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathbb C[[x]]/(x^m)=m.$$
For properly intersecting curves on a smooth surface, this quotient length gives the local multiplicity. Transversality gives multiplicity one; tangency can increase it.

For properly intersecting subvarieties $V,W$ in a smooth variety, at the generic point $\eta$ of an intersection component, the general formula is
$$\sum_{i\ge0}(-1)^i\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O_{X,\eta}}
\operatorname{Tor}^{\mathcal O_{X,\eta}}_i(\mathcal O_{V,\eta},\mathcal O_{W,\eta}).$$
Quotient length alone requires higher Tor contributions to vanish; it is not the general definition.
:::

::: {.front card-id="intersection-normal-cone-gysin" tags="construction comparison"}
How do normal cones and deformation to the normal cone define refined intersection? When is the normal cone a vector bundle?
:::

::: {.back card-id="intersection-normal-cone-gysin"}
For a closed immersion $Z\subset X$ with ideal $I$:
$$C_{Z/X}=\operatorname{Spec}_Z\bigoplus_{m\ge0}I^m/I^{m+1}.$$
If the immersion is regular of codimension $c$, then $C_{Z/X}=N_{Z/X}$, the rank-$c$ vector bundle dual to $I/I^2$.

Deformation to the normal cone uses the blowup of $X\times\mathbb A^1$ along $Z\times\{0\}$, removing the strict transform of $X\times\{0\}$. The general fiber is $X$ and the special fiber is $C_{Z/X}$. Specialize cycles to the cone, then pull back by the zero section of $N_{Z/X}$. After arbitrary base change this defines the refined Gysin map for the original regular immersion. This construction accounts for intersections that cannot be counted by isolated transverse points.
:::

::: {.front card-id="intersection-excess-self-intersection" tags="theorem application"}
State the excess-intersection formula for a clean intersection of smooth subvarieties. Recover self-intersection and apply it to a line in $\mathbb P^2$ and an exceptional curve.
:::

::: {.back card-id="intersection-excess-self-intersection"}
Let smooth $V,W\subset X$ meet in smooth $Z$, with $T_Z=T_V|_Z\cap T_W|_Z$. Put
$$F=T_X|_Z/(T_V|_Z+T_W|_Z),\qquad e=\dim Z-(\dim V+\dim W-\dim X).$$
Then $F$ has rank $e$ and $[V]\cdot[W]=j_*(c_e(F)\cap[Z])$, where $j:Z\hookrightarrow X$.

For a regular embedding $i:V\hookrightarrow X$ of codimension $c$:
$$i^!i_*\alpha=c_c(N_{V/X})\cap\alpha.$$
A line in $\mathbb P^2$ has normal bundle $\mathcal O(1)$ and square one. The exceptional curve of a point blowup has normal bundle $\mathcal O(-1)$ and square minus one. Neither self-intersection is a count of distinct points in the set-theoretic intersection.
:::

::: {.front card-id="intersection-chern-zero-locus" tags="theorem application"}
When does a section of a vector bundle compute a top Chern class? Count the zeros of a regular section of $\mathcal O(a)\oplus\mathcal O(b)$ on $\mathbb P^2$.
:::

::: {.back card-id="intersection-chern-zero-locus"}
For a rank-$r$ bundle $E$ on smooth $X$, if a section has zero scheme $Z$ of codimension $r$, then
$$[Z]=c_r(E)\cap[X].$$
If $Z$ is zero-dimensional, its degree counts scheme length; transverse zeros have length one. For $E=\mathcal O(a)\oplus\mathcal O(b)$ on $\mathbb P^2$:
$$c(E)=(1+aH)(1+bH),\qquad\deg Z=\int c_2(E)=ab.$$
This is Bézout for curves with no common component. A shared component violates expected codimension, so multiplying the degrees cannot count their isolated intersection points without excess corrections.
:::

::: {.front card-id="intersection-schubert-pieri-giambelli" tags="definition formula"}
Define Schubert classes on $\operatorname{Gr}(k,n)$ and state Pieri and Giambelli, fixing the partition convention.
:::

::: {.back card-id="intersection-schubert-pieri-giambelli"}
For a complete flag $F_\bullet$ and $n-k\ge\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_k\ge0$:
$$\Omega_\lambda(F)=\{V:\dim(V\cap F_{n-k+i-\lambda_i})\ge i,\ 1\le i\le k\},\qquad\sigma_\lambda=[\Omega_\lambda].$$
$\operatorname{codim}\Omega_\lambda=|\lambda|$. If $Q$ is the tautological quotient bundle, $\sigma_r=c_r(Q)$, with $\sigma_0=1$ and out-of-range $\sigma_r=0$.

Pieri:
$$\sigma_\lambda\sigma_r=\sum_\mu\sigma_\mu,$$
where $\mu/\lambda$ adds $r$ boxes with no two in the same column, staying in the rectangle. Giambelli:
$$\sigma_\lambda=\det(\sigma_{\lambda_i+j-i})_{1\le i,j\le k}.$$
The rectangular partition is the point class, of integral one. General flags give the incidence interpretation of the product.
:::

::: {.front card-id="intersection-four-lines" tags="application"}
How many lines in $\mathbb P^3_{\mathbb C}$ meet four general lines? Compute in the Chow ring and explain the genericity condition geometrically.
:::

::: {.back card-id="intersection-four-lines"}
Meeting a fixed line is the Schubert divisor $\sigma_1$ on $\operatorname{Gr}(2,4)$. Pieri gives
$$\sigma_1^2=\sigma_2+\sigma_{1,1},\qquad
\sigma_1^3=2\sigma_{2,1},\qquad\sigma_1^4=2\sigma_{2,2}.$$
The answer is two. Three general skew lines lie in one ruling of a smooth quadric; their transversals form the other ruling. A fourth general line meets the quadric in two distinct points, determining the two transversals. Tangency can merge them with multiplicity; a special line on the quadric can leave a positive-dimensional family.
:::

::: {.front card-id="intersection-cubic-lines-chern" tags="application"}
Compute the number of lines on a general smooth cubic surface in $\mathbb P^3_{\mathbb C}$ as a top Chern number on $\operatorname{Gr}(2,4)$.
:::

::: {.back card-id="intersection-cubic-lines-chern"}
Restrict the cubic equation to the tautological rank-two subbundle $S$: it gives a section of $\operatorname{Sym}^3S^*$, of rank four on the four-dimensional Grassmannian. For a general cubic its zeros are transverse.

For Chern roots $x,y$ of $S^*$:
$$c_4(\operatorname{Sym}^3S^*)=(3x)(2x+y)(x+2y)(3y)
=18xy(x+y)^2+9x^2y^2.$$
Now $xy=\sigma_{1,1}$, $x+y=\sigma_1$, and
$$\int\sigma_{1,1}\sigma_1^2=1,\qquad\int\sigma_{1,1}^2=1.$$
Thus the count is $18+9=27$. The parameter space, bundle rank, expected dimension, and transversality connect the integral to actual lines.
:::

::: {.front card-id="intersection-segre-blowup" tags="formula application"}
For the blowup $\pi:\widetilde X\to X$ of a smooth center $i:Z\hookrightarrow X$ of codimension $c\ge2$, express $\pi_*E^m$ using Segre classes. Apply it to the Veronese surface in $\mathbb P^5$.
:::

::: {.back card-id="intersection-segre-blowup"}
Use $s(N)=c(N)^{-1}$. For $1\le m<c$, $\pi_*E^m=0$, and for $j\ge0$:
$$\pi_*E^{c+j}=(-1)^{c+j-1}i_*s_j(N_{Z/X}).$$
For $Z=v_2(\mathbb P^2)\subset\mathbb P^5$, let $h$ be the plane hyperplane class and $H$ the pullback of the ambient hyperplane. Then $i^*H=2h$ and
$$c(N)=\frac{(1+2h)^6}{(1+h)^3}=1+9h+30h^2,\qquad s(N)=1-9h+51h^2.$$
Consequently
$$\int H^5=1,\quad\int H^4E=\int H^3E^2=0,\quad
\int H^2E^3=4,\quad\int HE^4=18,\quad\int E^5=51.$$
The signs come from $\mathcal O_E(E)=\mathcal O_E(-1)$, not from treating $E$ as a positive hyperplane class.
:::

::: {.front card-id="intersection-3264-complete-conics" tags="application construction" cite="[@EH16, complete conics; @Koc02]"}
How many smooth plane conics over $\mathbb C$ are tangent to five general smooth conics? Explain the failure of naive Bézout and compute the corrected intersection on the space of complete conics.
:::

::: {.back card-id="intersection-3264-complete-conics"}
Plane conics form $\mathbb P^5$. Tangency to one fixed smooth conic is a sextic hypersurface: restrict the variable quadratic form to that conic to obtain a binary quartic, whose discriminant has degree six in its coefficients. All such sextics contain the Veronese surface $V$ of double lines with multiplicity two.

Blow up $V$: $\widetilde{\mathbb P^5}=\operatorname{Bl}_V\mathbb P^5$, the space of complete conics. With $H$ the pullback hyperplane and $E$ exceptional, the strict tangency class is $6H-2E$. General tangency conditions meet transversely in the smooth-conic locus. Using
$$\int(H^5,H^4E,H^3E^2,H^2E^3,HE^4,E^5)=(1,0,0,4,18,51),$$
one obtains
$$\int(6H-2E)^5=7776-11520+8640-1632=3264.$$
Naive $6^5$ counts a nonproper intersection with the shared double-line locus. The blowup removes that excess contribution while recording limiting tangency data.
:::
