::: {.front card-id="card-d88dc079b7f19264" tags="definition"}
What is: Affine varieties
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-d88dc079b7f19264"}
An affine variety over $k$ is an integral separated scheme of finite type isomorphic to $\operatorname{Spec}A$ for a finitely generated $k$-domain $A$. Over an algebraically closed field, it can be realized as the common zero locus of polynomials in $\mathbb A_k^n$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-1d238038a4983580" tags="definition"}
What is: Projective varieties
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-1d238038a4983580"}
A projective variety over $k$ is an integral closed subscheme of $\mathbb P_k^n$ for some $n$. Equivalently, it is $\operatorname{Proj}S$ for a suitable finitely generated graded $k$-domain generated in degree one.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-9dfd657ee639cc46" tags="unsorted"}
What is $\PP^n/S$ for $S$ a base scheme?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-9dfd657ee639cc46"}
Define $\PP^n/\ZZ \da \Proj \ZZ[x_0, \cdots, x_n]$ and $\PP^n/S \da \PP^n/\ZZ \fiberprod{\ZZ} S$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-d0d7557976a8bb37" tags="unsorted"}
Show that $\PP^n$ is proper.
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-d0d7557976a8bb37"}
For a valuation ring $R$ with fraction field $K$, a map $\operatorname{Spec}K\to\mathbb P_R^n$ is given by homogeneous coordinates $[a_0:\cdots:a_n]$ in $K$. Scale them so all $a_i\in R$ and at least one is a unit. They then define an extension $\operatorname{Spec}R\to\mathbb P_R^n$. Separatedness gives uniqueness. The valuative criterion proves $\mathbb P_R^n\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ is proper; hence $\mathbb P_k^n$ is proper over $k$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-c2dcd4397ae86227" tags="definition"}
What is: Morphisms
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-c2dcd4397ae86227"}
A morphism of schemes $f:X\to Y$ consists of a continuous map and a map of sheaves $f^\#:\mathcal O_Y\to f_*\mathcal O_X$ such that each induced map on stalks $\mathcal O_{Y,f(x)}\to\mathcal O_{X,x}$ is local. For varieties over $k$, the morphism also respects the maps to $\operatorname{Spec}k$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-3b757b5adf06e626" tags="definition"}
What is: Rational maps
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-3b757b5adf06e626"}
For integral varieties $X,Y$, a rational map $X\dashrightarrow Y$ is an equivalence class of morphisms $U\to Y$ on nonempty open subsets $U\subseteq X$. Two agree when they agree on a common dense open subset. It is dominant if its image is dense in $Y$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-b7fee6cc99998ad4" tags="definition"}
What is: Nonsingular varieties
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-b7fee6cc99998ad4"}
A variety $X$ is nonsingular at $x$ if its local ring $\mathcal O_{X,x}$ is regular: the minimal number of generators of its maximal ideal equals $\dim\mathcal O_{X,x}$. It is nonsingular if this holds at every point.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-aeb31f26f04430b2" tags="definition"}
What is: Nonsingular curves
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-aeb31f26f04430b2"}
A nonsingular curve is a one-dimensional variety whose local rings are regular. At every closed point of an integral nonsingular curve, the local ring is a discrete valuation ring; divisors there are sums of closed points.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-6740aaf8254f55aa" tags="definition"}
What is: Presheaves
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-6740aaf8254f55aa"}
A presheaf $F$ on $X$ assigns an object $F(U)$ to each open $U\subseteq X$ and a restriction map $F(U)\to F(V)$ for $V\subseteq U$, with identity restrictions and compatibility under composition. A sheaf also satisfies gluing and uniqueness for compatible local sections.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-957476fd59afd34c" tags="definition"}
What is: sheafification
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-957476fd59afd34c"}
The sheafification $F^+$ of a presheaf $F$ is a sheaf with a map $F\to F^+$ that induces isomorphisms on all stalks. Every map from $F$ to a sheaf factors uniquely through $F^+$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-fef610387edab7ba" tags="definition"}
What is: morphisms of (pre)sheaves
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-fef610387edab7ba"}
A morphism $\varphi:F\to G$ of presheaves is a family of maps $\varphi_U:F(U)\to G(U)$ commuting with every restriction map. A morphism of sheaves is the same kind of compatible family between sheaves.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-9f13fac2ccdb8843" tags="definition"}
What is: pullback and pushforward
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-9f13fac2ccdb8843"}
For $f:X\to Y$ and a sheaf $F$ on $X$, $(f_*F)(V)=F(f^{-1}V)$. For a sheaf $G$ on $Y$, $f^{-1}G$ is the sheafification of $U\mapsto\varinjlim_{U\subseteq f^{-1}V}G(V)$. For modules on ringed spaces, $f^*G=f^{-1}G\otimes_{f^{-1}\mathcal O_Y}\mathcal O_X$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-fddda97ba09ecea5" tags="definition"}
What is: Skyscraper sheaves
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-fddda97ba09ecea5"}
For a point $x\in X$ and an abelian group $A$, the skyscraper sheaf $i_*A$ for $i:\{x\}\hookrightarrow X$ has sections $A$ on opens containing $x$ and $0$ on other opens. Restrictions are the evident identity or zero maps.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-b218c782bf9abe54" tags="definition"}
What is: constant sheaves
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-b218c782bf9abe54"}
The constant sheaf $\underline A$ is the sheafification of the constant presheaf $U\mapsto A$. Its sections on $U$ are locally constant functions $U\to A$ when $A$ has the discrete topology.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-513951922e04ccb4" tags="definition"}
What is: sheaf hom
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-513951922e04ccb4"}
For $\mathcal O_X$-modules $F,G$, the sheaf $\mathcal Hom_{\mathcal O_X}(F,G)$ has sections $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_U}(F|_U,G|_U)$ on each open $U\subseteq X$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-b5dfbf872b61db5b" tags="definition"}
What is: sheaf kernel/cokernel
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-b5dfbf872b61db5b"}
The kernel of $F\to G$ is the subsheaf $U\mapsto\ker(F(U)\to G(U))$. The cokernel is the sheafification of $U\mapsto\operatorname{coker}(F(U)\to G(U))$; a section of the sheaf cokernel need only have local representatives.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-fae8e7095295f561" tags="definition"}
What is: Ringed spaces and their morphisms
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-fae8e7095295f561"}
A ringed space is a topological space $X$ with a sheaf of rings $\mathcal O_X$. A morphism $(X,\mathcal O_X)\to(Y,\mathcal O_Y)$ is a continuous map $f$ with a sheaf map $f^\#:\mathcal O_Y\to f_*\mathcal O_X$. It is locally ringed if the stalk maps are local homomorphisms.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-943a2df5418e9de9" tags="definition"}
What is: the structure sheaf
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-943a2df5418e9de9"}
The structure sheaf $\mathcal O_X$ records regular functions locally on a scheme. On $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$, $\mathcal O_X(D(f))=A_f$, and the stalk at $\mathfrak p$ is $A_{\mathfrak p}$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-8b388f202394fece" tags="definition"}
What is: Generic points and closed points
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-8b388f202394fece"}
A point $\eta$ is generic for a closed subset $Z$ if $\overline{\{\eta\}}=Z$. A point $x$ is closed if $\{x\}$ is closed. In $\operatorname{Spec}A$, $\mathfrak p$ is closed exactly when it is maximal, and the generic point of $V(\mathfrak p)$ is $\mathfrak p$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-3994ac4fe8600330" tags="definition"}
What is: residue fields
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-3994ac4fe8600330"}
The residue field at $x\in X$ is $\kappa(x)=\mathcal O_{X,x}/\mathfrak m_x$. For $x=\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}A$, it is the fraction field of $A/\mathfrak p$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-03a12cd80ccb7400" tags="definition"}
What is: Definitions of affine/projective/general schemes
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-03a12cd80ccb7400"}
An affine scheme is $\operatorname{Spec}A$ for a ring $A$. A scheme is a locally ringed space covered by open affine schemes. A projective scheme over $S$ admits a closed immersion into $\mathbb P_S^n$ for some $n$; the term also has a relative $\operatorname{Proj}$ formulation.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-669784a99e1324c8" tags="definition"}
What is: Proj
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-669784a99e1324c8"}
For a nonnegatively graded ring $S$, $\operatorname{Proj}S$ has points the homogeneous prime ideals not containing $S_+=\bigoplus_{d>0}S_d$. Its basic opens satisfy $D_+(f)\simeq\operatorname{Spec}(S_f)_0$ for homogeneous $f$ of positive degree.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-67c4242b2b221ce9" tags="definition"}
What is: Morphisms of finite type
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-67c4242b2b221ce9"}
A morphism $f:X\to Y$ is locally of finite type if every point of $X$ has affine neighborhoods $\operatorname{Spec}B\subseteq f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A)$ with $B$ a finitely generated $A$-algebra. It is of finite type if it is also quasi-compact.

What is: finite morphisms
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-8545991b9f8fb473"}
A morphism $f:X\to Y$ is finite if locally on affine opens $V=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq Y$, $f^{-1}(V)=\operatorname{Spec}B$ with $B$ a finite $A$-module.
:::

:::

::: {.back card-id="card-f8d31bfb2abb0b49"}
An open immersion identifies $X$ with an open subscheme of $Y$. A closed immersion identifies $X$ with a closed subscheme of $Y$; locally it comes from a surjection of rings $A\twoheadrightarrow A/I$.

:::

::: {.back card-id="card-0698c886db4b9a7c"}
A morphism $f:X\to Y$ is affine if the inverse image of every affine open in $Y$ is affine. It is projective if it factors as a closed immersion $X\hookrightarrow\mathbb P_Y^n$ followed by projection, for some $n$.

What is: Dimension
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-4ce6d430cdf6d189"}
The dimension of a scheme $X$ is the supremum of lengths of strict chains of irreducible closed subsets of $X$. For $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$, this is the Krull dimension of $A$.

What is: Krull dimension
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-978a7dee07c6ba08"}
The Krull dimension of a ring $A$ is the supremum of lengths $n$ of chains of prime ideals $\mathfrak p_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak p_n$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-5cbae70edc215135" tags="definition"}
What is: Fiber products
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-5cbae70edc215135"}

What is: Reduced
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-c36e3e181d644cc2"}
A scheme is reduced if every stalk $\mathcal O_{X,x}$ has no nonzero nilpotent. Equivalently, every $\mathcal O_X(U)$ is reduced.

What is: irreducible
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-43d517912e57982d"}
A scheme is irreducible if its underlying topological space is nonempty and cannot be written as the union of two proper closed subsets. Equivalently, any two nonempty open subsets meet.
:::

What is: integral
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-a518e09076d21766"}
A scheme is integral if it is nonempty, reduced, and irreducible. Equivalently, each nonempty affine open is the spectrum of a domain.

::: {.front card-id="card-24e6d1c8282799dd" tags="definition"}
What is: Definition of separated and proper morphisms,
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-24e6d1c8282799dd"}
We say a variety $X$ is separated if the image of the diagonal map $\Delta: X \rightarrow X \times X$ is Zariski closed in $X \times X$.

For instance, $\mathbb{C}^n$ is separated because the image of the diagonal in $\mathbb{C}^n \times \mathbb{C}^n=$ $\operatorname{Spec}\left(\mathbb{C}\left[x_1, \ldots, x_n, y_1, \ldots, y_n\right]\right)$ is the affine variety $\mathbf{V}\left(x_1-y_1, \ldots, x_n-y_n\right)$. Similarly, any affine variety is separated.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-7e03d8abaf3d84b6" tags="unsorted"}
What is a proper morphism? What are some consequences?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-7e03d8abaf3d84b6"}
Definitions: 

- A morphism of schemes is proper if it is universally closed, separated, of finite type.

- A morphism of algebraic varieties $\phi: X \rightarrow Y$ is proper if it is universally closed, i.e., if for all $\psi: Z \rightarrow Y$, the mapping $\phi^{\prime}: X \times \times_Y Z \rightarrow Z$ is a closed mapping in the Zariski topology.

Important things to note about properness:

(a) $X$ complete iff the structure morphism to a point is proper.
(b) Fibers of a proper morphism are complete.
(c) Inverse images of complete subvarieties of $Y$ are complete.
(d) Any morphism obtained from base change from a proper morphism is automatically proper
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-e1a99de59c6e75bf" tags="unsorted"}
When is a toric variety Calabi-Yau?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-e1a99de59c6e75bf"}
If the fan $\Sigma$ of $X_{\Sigma}$ has all its primitive generators lying on a hyperplane of $N_{\mathbb{R}}$, then $X_{\Sigma}$ is Calabi-Yau.

Proof. Let $D_i$ be all the toric divisors with corresponding primitive generators $u_i$. If all the primitive generators lie on a hyperplane, then we can choose a basis for $N$ such that the first coordinate of each of them is 1 , i.e. $u_{i n}=1$. But then:
$$
0 \sim \operatorname{div}\left(\chi^{e_1}\right)=\left\langle e_1, u_1\right\rangle D_1+\cdots+\left\langle e_1, u_r\right\rangle D_r=\sum D_i .
$$
Hence, since $X_{\Sigma}$ is toric,
$$
\mathcal{K} \cong \mathcal{O}_{X_{\Sigma}}\left(-\sum D_i\right) \cong \mathcal{O}_{X_{\Sigma}},
$$
where $\mathcal{O}_X(D)$ is the sheaf of the Weil divisor $D$. Consequently the canonical divisor is trivial and $X_{\Sigma}$ is Calabi-Yau.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-e1b36c9f1c246df3" tags="definition"}
What is: Valuative criteria for properness
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-e1b36c9f1c246df3"}
For $f:X\to Y$ of finite type, consider every valuation ring $R$ with fraction field $K$ and every commutative square $\operatorname{Spec}K\to X$, $\operatorname{Spec}R\to Y$. The map $f$ is proper exactly when a unique lift $\operatorname{Spec}R\to X$ exists for every such square. Existence tests universal closedness; uniqueness tests separatedness.
:::

:::

::: {.back card-id="card-1147ea91c75c2c87"}

- Universally closed: $\geq 1$ way to fill.
- Proper: $=1$ way to fill.

What is: Projective morphisms,
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-b8b844b9ac724909"}
A morphism of schemes $f: X \longrightarrow Y$ is projective if it factors as $X \stackrel{f^{\prime}}{\longrightarrow} \mathbf{P}_Y^n \stackrel{\pi}{\longrightarrow} Y$ for some $n$ where $f^{\prime}$ is a closed immersion.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-20137ed3c8a6a129" tags="definition"}
What is: Reduced scheme structure of a closed subset,
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-20137ed3c8a6a129"}
For a closed subset $Z\subseteq X$, there is a unique reduced closed subscheme $Z_{\mathrm{red}}\hookrightarrow X$ with underlying space $Z$. On an affine open $\operatorname{Spec}A$, it is cut out by the radical ideal of functions vanishing on $Z$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-18782c5a0371defb" tags="definition"}
What is: Scheme-theoretic image,
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-18782c5a0371defb"}
For a quasi-compact morphism $f:X\to Y$, the scheme-theoretic image is the smallest closed subscheme of $Y$ through which $f$ factors. It is defined by $\ker(\mathcal O_Y\to f_*\mathcal O_X)$ when this kernel is quasi-coherent, as for a quasi-compact quasi-separated morphism.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-17b2923b70fbbe6c" tags="definition"}
What is: Definition of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules,
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-17b2923b70fbbe6c"}
An $\mathcal O_X$-module is a sheaf $F$ of abelian groups such that each $F(U)$ is an $\mathcal O_X(U)$-module and all restriction maps respect scalar multiplication.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-37be5dabde736ed0" tags="definition"}
What is: Quasi-coherent and coherent sheaves,
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-37be5dabde736ed0"}
An $\mathcal O_X$-module $F$ is quasi-coherent if every point has an affine neighborhood $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ with $F|_U\cong\widetilde M$ for an $A$-module $M$. On a locally Noetherian scheme, $F$ is coherent if locally $M$ is a finite $A$-module.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-eeb89ede8813d491" tags="definition"}
What is: Construction of $\mathcal{O}_X$-modules,
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-eeb89ede8813d491"}
For an $A$-module $M$ on $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$, the associated sheaf $\widetilde M$ is determined by $\widetilde M(D(f))=M_f$. More general $\mathcal O_X$-modules are obtained by gluing modules on open subsets with compatible isomorphisms on overlaps.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-4cd160edee37a085" tags="definition"}
What is: Invertible sheaves and vector bundles
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-4cd160edee37a085"}
An invertible sheaf is an $\mathcal O_X$-module locally isomorphic to $\mathcal O_X$; it is a locally free sheaf of rank one. A vector bundle of rank $r$ is a scheme over $X$ locally isomorphic to $\mathbb A_X^r$ with linear transition maps; it corresponds to a locally free sheaf of rank $r$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-a42a3a6b58b697f2" tags="definition"}
What is: Cartier divisors and their associated invertible sheaves;
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-a42a3a6b58b697f2"}
On an integral scheme, a Cartier divisor is given by local nonzero rational functions $f_i$ with $f_i/f_j\in\mathcal O_X^\times$ on overlaps. The associated invertible subsheaf of the rational function sheaf is locally $f_i^{-1}\mathcal O_X$, denoted $\mathcal O_X(D)$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-7267264567570244" tags="definition"}
What is: Multiplicity and support of Cartier divisors;
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-7267264567570244"}
For a Cartier divisor $D$ on a regular integral scheme, its coefficient at a codimension-one point $\eta$ is the valuation $\operatorname{ord}_\eta(f)$ of a local equation $f$. Its support is the union of codimension-one subvarieties with nonzero coefficient. More generally, the support is where a local equation is not a unit.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-2762c1507cb47d71" tags="definition"}
What is: Cartier divisors of rational sections of invertible sheaves;
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-2762c1507cb47d71"}
A nonzero rational section $s$ of an invertible sheaf $L$ defines a Cartier divisor: write $s=f_i e_i$ in each local frame $e_i$ and use the rational functions $f_i$ as local equations. With the convention $L\cong\mathcal O_X(D)$, the canonical rational section of $\mathcal O_X(D)$ has divisor $D$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-e15cea224fd5fb22" tags="definition"}
What is: Weil divisors and relation with Cartier (equivalence for locally factorial schemes)
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-e15cea224fd5fb22"}
A Weil divisor on a Noetherian integral scheme is a finite integral sum of codimension-one integral closed subschemes. A Cartier divisor determines a Weil divisor by its orders at codimension-one points. On a locally factorial scheme this map is an isomorphism of divisor groups.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-6f854b0e887f821f" tags="definition"}
What is: The Picard group and class group
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-6f854b0e887f821f"}
$\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ is the group of invertible sheaves up to isomorphism under tensor product. For a normal integral Noetherian scheme, $\operatorname{Cl}(X)$ is the group of Weil divisors modulo principal divisors. Cartier divisors modulo principal divisors give $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$; on a locally factorial scheme, $\operatorname{Pic}(X)\cong\operatorname{Cl}(X)$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-60e9dfcc9184fa85" tags="definition"}
What is: Degree of a divisor
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-60e9dfcc9184fa85"}
For a divisor $D=\sum_P n_P[P]$ on a proper curve over $k$, $\deg D=\sum_P n_P[\kappa(P):k]$. Over an algebraically closed field, this is $\sum_P n_P$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-1a2f4f4d5b245d5a" tags="definition"}
What is: Ample and very ample sheaves,
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-1a2f4f4d5b245d5a"}
An invertible sheaf $L$ is very ample over $S$ if it gives an immersion $X\hookrightarrow\mathbb P_S^n$ with $L\cong i^*\mathcal O(1)$. It is ample if every point has an affine neighborhood of the form $X_s=\{s\ne0\}$ for some global section of some positive power $L^{\otimes n}$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-8ec58f09e82af130" tags="definition"}
What is: Blowups
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-8ec58f09e82af130"}
The blowup of a scheme $X$ along an ideal sheaf $I$ is $\operatorname{Bl}_I X=\operatorname{Proj}_X\bigoplus_{n\geq0}I^n$. It is universal among maps $Y\to X$ for which the pulled-back ideal $I\mathcal O_Y$ is invertible.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-f9e01cd1f2afecc6" tags="definition"}
What is: Derivations
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-f9e01cd1f2afecc6"}
A $k$-derivation $D:A\to M$ is $k$-linear and satisfies $D(ab)=aD(b)+bD(a)$. It kills the image of $k$ in $A$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-cbfb24232131f375" tags="definition"}
What is: module of relative differential forms
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-cbfb24232131f375"}
The module of Kähler differentials $\Omega_{A/R}$ is generated by symbols $da$ subject to additivity, $d(ab)=a\,db+b\,da$, and $dr=0$ for $r\in R$. Its universal property is $\operatorname{Hom}_A(\Omega_{A/R},M)\cong\operatorname{Der}_R(A,M)$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-28213da6a96eb9ed" tags="definition"}
What is: Tangent sheaf
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-28213da6a96eb9ed"}
For $f:X\to S$, the relative tangent sheaf is $\mathcal T_{X/S}=\mathcal Hom_{\mathcal O_X}(\Omega^1_{X/S},\mathcal O_X)$. Its local sections are $\mathcal O_S$-linear derivations of $\mathcal O_X$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-1af69ef40541b9cb" tags="definition"}
What is: Algebraic differential forms;
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-1af69ef40541b9cb"}
Algebraic differential $p$-forms are sections of $\Omega^p_{X/S}=\bigwedge^p\Omega^1_{X/S}$. The exterior derivative $d:\Omega^p_{X/S}\to\Omega^{p+1}_{X/S}$ satisfies $d^2=0$ and the graded Leibniz rule.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-594fc45f771e7003" tags="definition"}
What is: Dualizing sheaf and Serre duality
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-594fc45f771e7003"}
For a smooth proper connected curve $X/k$, the dualizing sheaf is $\omega_X=\Omega^1_{X/k}$. Serre duality gives $H^1(X,L)^\vee\cong H^0(X,\omega_X\otimes L^{-1})$ for every invertible sheaf $L$. In dimension $n$, it pairs $H^i(X,F)$ with $\operatorname{Ext}^{n-i}(F,\omega_X)$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-65cfb8efb1626b1d" tags="definition"}
What is: relation to the canonical sheaf
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-65cfb8efb1626b1d"}
On a smooth $n$-dimensional variety over a field, the canonical sheaf is $\omega_X=\bigwedge^n\Omega^1_{X/k}$, which is also the dualizing sheaf. On a singular scheme the dualizing object need not be an invertible sheaf.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-136accbe28d1a749" tags="definition"}
What is: The (co)normal sheaf,
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-136accbe28d1a749"}
For a closed immersion $i:Z\hookrightarrow X$ with ideal sheaf $I$, the conormal sheaf is $I/I^2$. Its dual $\mathcal Hom_{\mathcal O_Z}(I/I^2,\mathcal O_Z)$ is the normal sheaf. For smooth $Z$ and $X$, the conormal sequence is $I/I^2\to i^*\Omega_X^1\to\Omega_Z^1\to0$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-006c2199efa06a65" tags="definition"}
What is: Regular immersions and local complete intersections;
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-006c2199efa06a65"}
A closed immersion $Z\hookrightarrow X$ is regular of codimension $c$ if its ideal is locally generated by a regular sequence of length $c$; then $I/I^2$ is locally free of rank $c$. A local complete intersection morphism factors locally as a regular immersion followed by a smooth morphism.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-870c96f4b6081b54" tags="definition"}
What is: Normal schemes and normalization
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-870c96f4b6081b54"}
A ring $R$ with field of fractions $K$ is **normal** if every element of $K$ which is integral over $R$ (i.e. a root of monic polynomial in $R[x]$ ) is actually in $R$. 

A variety $X$ is **normal** if it is irreducible and the local rings $\mathcal{O}_{X, p}$ are normal for all $p \in X$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-c02b908a88e673d4" tags="definition"}
What is: the adjunction formula
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-c02b908a88e673d4"}
For a regular immersion $i:Z\hookrightarrow X$ of codimension $c$ between smooth varieties, $\omega_Z\cong i^*\omega_X\otimes\det(N_{Z/X})$. For a smooth divisor $D\subset X$, this is $K_D=(K_X+D)|_D$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-8c26bb201aefe21c" tags="definition"}
What is: crepant resolutions
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-8c26bb201aefe21c"}
A resolution $f:Y\to X$ of a normal $\mathbb Q$-Gorenstein variety is crepant if $K_Y=f^*K_X$ as $\mathbb Q$-divisors, equivalently if all exceptional discrepancies are zero.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-69338eadccabdac7" tags="definition"}
What is: Geometric and arithmetic genus
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-69338eadccabdac7"}
For a proper integral curve $C$ over an algebraically closed field, the arithmetic genus is $p_a(C)=1-\chi(\mathcal O_C)=h^1(C,\mathcal O_C)$. The geometric genus is $g(C)=h^1(\widetilde C,\mathcal O_{\widetilde C})$ for the normalization $\widetilde C$; they agree when $C$ is smooth.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-8c987eee74af2624" tags="definition"}
What is: Riemann-Roch
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-8c987eee74af2624"}
For a divisor $D$ on a smooth projective curve $C$ of genus $g$, Riemann–Roch says $h^0(C,\mathcal O_C(D))-h^0(C,\mathcal O_C(K_C-D))=\deg D+1-g$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-b6a6951f917ad2a7" tags="definition"}
What is: Riemann-Hurwitz
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-b6a6951f917ad2a7"}
Consider a finite morphism of curves $f: X \longrightarrow Y$ of degree $n=[K(X): K(Y)]$. If $f$ is separable, we have $2 g(X)-2=n(2 g(Y)-2)+\operatorname{deg} R$ where $R=$ $\sum_P$ (length $\left.\Omega_{X / Y}\right)_P[P]$ is the ramification divisor. If $f$ is purely inseparable, then $g(X)=g(Y)$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-d3ffa1d6f16c5059" tags="definition"}
What is: The dual character/cocharacter lattices M and N,
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-d3ffa1d6f16c5059"}
For a torus $T$, the character lattice is $M=\operatorname{Hom}(T,\mathbb G_m)$ and the cocharacter lattice is $N=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb G_m,T)$. They are dual free abelian groups, paired by $\chi^m\circ\lambda^n(t)=t^{\langle m,n\rangle}$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-5eaec241ffdf1c8d" tags="definition"}
What is: Strongly convex rational polyhedral cones and their dual cones
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-5eaec241ffdf1c8d"}
A rational polyhedral cone $\sigma\subset N_{\mathbb R}$ is generated by finitely many lattice vectors. It is strongly convex if $\sigma\cap(-\sigma)=\{0\}$. Its dual cone is $\sigma^\vee=\{m\in M_{\mathbb R}:\langle m,n\rangle\geq0\text{ for all }n\in\sigma\}$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-0f0b490e51410393" tags="definition"}
What is: Constructing affine toric varieties from semigroup algebras
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-0f0b490e51410393"}
For a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone $\sigma\subset N_{\mathbb R}$, put $S_\sigma=\sigma^\vee\cap M$. The affine toric variety is $U_\sigma=\operatorname{Spec}k[S_\sigma]$, where $\chi^m\chi^{m'}=\chi^{m+m'}$. Its dense torus is $\operatorname{Spec}k[M]$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-97656fa2933aad33" tags="definition"}
What is: and projective toric varieties from gluing affines
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-97656fa2933aad33"}
A fan $\Sigma$ gives affine charts $U_\sigma=\operatorname{Spec}k[\sigma^\vee\cap M]$. If $\tau=\sigma\cap\sigma'$ is a common face, identify the open chart $U_\tau$ in $U_\sigma$ and $U_{\sigma'}$. Gluing all charts gives $X_\Sigma$. A lattice polytope supplies a projective example through its normal fan and associated ample divisor.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-3a42e7b57789923a" tags="definition"}
What is: Toric varieties from polytopes
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-3a42e7b57789923a"}
A full-dimensional lattice polytope $P\subset M_{\mathbb R}$ defines a projective toric variety $X_P$ from its normal fan. The lattice points $m\in P\cap M$ index monomial sections $\chi^m$ of the associated ample line bundle. If the bundle is very ample, these sections give the projective embedding.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-6baf2a09b22ee77f" tags="definition"}
What is: polar duals
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-6baf2a09b22ee77f"}
For a full-dimensional polytope $P\subset M_{\mathbb R}$ containing $0$ in its interior, its polar dual is $P^\circ=\{n\in N_{\mathbb R}:\langle m,n\rangle\geq-1\text{ for every }m\in P\}$. The sign convention places facets of a reflexive polytope at lattice distance one from $0$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-805563e57f835751" tags="definition"}
What is: reflexive polytopes
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-805563e57f835751"}
A lattice polytope $P$ is reflexive if $0$ is in its interior and its polar dual $P^\circ$ is also a lattice polytope. Equivalently, every facet of $P$ has an equation $\langle m,n_F\rangle=-1$ with primitive lattice normal $n_F$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-0b743a401cc51f17" tags="definition"}
What is: The orbit-cone correspondence
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-0b743a401cc51f17"}
Cones $\sigma\in\Sigma$ correspond bijectively to torus orbits $O(\sigma)\subset X_\Sigma$, with $\dim O(\sigma)=\dim N_{\mathbb R}-\dim\sigma$. The closure relation reverses face inclusion: $O(\tau)\subseteq\overline{O(\sigma)}$ exactly when $\sigma$ is a face of $\tau$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-168d61368865c14d" tags="definition"}
What is: orbit closures
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-168d61368865c14d"}
The orbit closure $V(\sigma)=\overline{O(\sigma)}$ is itself a toric variety for the quotient lattice $N/(N\cap\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\sigma)$ and the star fan of $\sigma$. It has codimension $\dim\sigma$ in $X_\Sigma$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-cc0dd9847aa22865" tags="definition"}
What is: Toric morphisms
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-cc0dd9847aa22865"}
A lattice map $F:N\to N'$ defines a toric morphism $X_\Sigma\to X_{\Sigma'}$ if for every $\sigma\in\Sigma$, some $\sigma'\in\Sigma'$ contains $F_{\mathbb R}(\sigma)$. On tori, the map is induced by the dual character map $F^*:M'\to M$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-e2f27902fea3fec2" tags="definition"}
What is: Checking smoothness
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-e2f27902fea3fec2"}
Let $\sigma \subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}$ be a SCRPC,

- $\sigma$ is **smooth** if its minimal generators form part of a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis for $N$.
- $\sigma$ is **simplicial** if its minimal generators are linearly independent over $N_{\mathbb{R}}$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-7593e551f96eb2a1" tags="examples"}
Give an example of a simplicial cone that is not smooth.
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-7593e551f96eb2a1"}
In $N=\mathbb Z^2$, the cone $\sigma=\operatorname{Cone}((1,0),(1,2))$ is simplicial because its generators are linearly independent. It is not smooth because their determinant is $2$, so they do not form a lattice basis.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-a43b485cd1920201" tags="definition"}
What is: properness/completeness
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-a43b485cd1920201"}
A separated variety is **complete** iff universally closed, or equivalently its structure morphism $X\to \spec k$ is proper.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-316d069b2fb8fdc1" tags="definition"}
What is: Sufficient conditions for orbifold/quotient singularities
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-316d069b2fb8fdc1"}
If every cone of a fan is simplicial, its toric variety has finite quotient singularities: each affine chart is a quotient of a smooth toric chart by a finite abelian group. A cone is smooth precisely when its primitive ray generators extend to a basis of $N$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-cee534a5bc1e4d70" tags="definition"}
What is: Characterization of (very) ample line bundles
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-cee534a5bc1e4d70"}
For a complete toric variety and a torus-invariant Cartier divisor $D$, $D$ is ample exactly when its support function is strictly convex. It is globally generated exactly when the support function is convex. Very ampleness requires, in addition, that lattice points of the divisor polytope generate each local semigroup at a vertex; on a smooth complete toric variety, ample implies very ample.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-0a7c3dc0fc3dba52" tags="definition"}
What is: Sections of line bundles from lattice points of rational convex polyhedra
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-0a7c3dc0fc3dba52"}
For $D=\sum_\rho a_\rho D_\rho$, set $P_D=\{m\in M_{\mathbb R}:\langle m,u_\rho\rangle\geq-a_\rho\text{ for all rays }\rho\}$. Then $H^0(X_\Sigma,\mathcal O(D))=\bigoplus_{m\in P_D\cap M}k\chi^m$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-d8ea8076516d498a" tags="definition"}
What is: Classification of complete nonsingular toric surfaces
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-d8ea8076516d498a"}
Every smooth complete toric surface comes from $\mathbb P^2$ or a Hirzebruch surface $\mathbb F_a$ by a sequence of blowups at torus-fixed points. In fan terms, a toric blowup of a fixed point inserts the ray generated by the sum of the adjacent primitive ray generators.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-ee0fa130f651a1f1" tags="definition"}
What is: Classification of smooth del Pezzo surfaces
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-ee0fa130f651a1f1"}
Over an algebraically closed field, the smooth del Pezzo surfaces are $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$ and the blowups of $\mathbb P^2$ at $r\leq8$ points in general position. The smooth *toric* del Pezzo surfaces are $\mathbb P^2$, $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$, and the toric blowups of $\mathbb P^2$ at one, two, or three torus-fixed points.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-ddbc3c3742e680c4" tags="definition"}
What is: Blowups and resolution of singularities
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-ddbc3c3742e680c4"}
For a smooth toric variety, blowing up the orbit closure $V(\sigma)$ corresponds to star subdivision of the fan at the ray through the sum of the primitive generators of $\sigma$. In dimension two, inserting rays between adjacent smooth rays resolves toric surface singularities after enough subdivisions.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-eb3e0bbc14ea7a20" tags="definition"}
What is: Weil divisors associated to rays
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-eb3e0bbc14ea7a20"}
Each ray $\rho\in\Sigma(1)$ determines the torus-invariant prime Weil divisor $D_\rho=V(\rho)$, the closure of its codimension-one orbit. Every torus-invariant Weil divisor is an integral sum $\sum_\rho a_\rho D_\rho$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-37e7bec8e1234876" tags="definition"}
What is: Cartier divisors associated to integral piecewise-linear support functions
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-37e7bec8e1234876"}
An integral piecewise-linear support function $\psi$ is linear on each cone $\sigma$, say $\psi|_\sigma=\langle m_\sigma,\cdot\rangle$ for $m_\sigma\in M$. It defines the torus-invariant Cartier divisor $D_\psi=-\sum_\rho\psi(u_\rho)D_\rho$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-eca8b37a5447d4e6" tags="definition"}
What is: Global sections of line bundles
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-eca8b37a5447d4e6"}
For a torus-invariant divisor $D=\sum a_\rho D_\rho$, the monomial $\chi^m$ is a global section of $\mathcal O(D)$ exactly when $\langle m,u_\rho\rangle+a_\rho\geq0$ for every ray. Thus $H^0(X,\mathcal O(D))$ has basis $\{\chi^m:m\in P_D\cap M\}$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-b2174371b4406220" tags="definition"}
What is: Canonical divisors of toric varieties
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-b2174371b4406220"}
The torus-invariant canonical divisor of a normal toric variety is $K_{X_\Sigma}=-\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}D_\rho$. It is Cartier exactly when, on each cone, there is $m_\sigma\in M$ pairing to $1$ with every primitive ray generator of that cone.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-aa64d6309faccb8f" tags="definition"}
What is: Fano toric varieties
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-aa64d6309faccb8f"}
A toric variety is Fano if it is projective and $-K_X$ is ample. For a Gorenstein toric Fano variety, the primitive ray generators form the vertices of a reflexive polytope; smooth toric Fano varieties correspond to smooth Fano polytopes.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-3eec3924ff2ebf24" tags="definition"}
What is: Fundamental groups and Euler characteristics,
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-3eec3924ff2ebf24"}
For a smooth complete complex toric variety $X_\Sigma$, $\pi_1(X_\Sigma)=0$ and its Euler characteristic is the number of maximal cones of $\Sigma$, equal to the number of torus-fixed points. The Euler count follows because positive-dimensional torus orbits have Euler characteristic zero.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-f63abdd3dfd856a2" tags="definition"}
What is: T-Cartier and T-Weil divisors and their intersection numbers
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-f63abdd3dfd856a2"}
Torus-invariant Weil divisors are sums $\sum a_\rho D_\rho$; such a divisor is Cartier exactly when its coefficients come from integral linear functions on the cones. On a smooth complete toric surface, adjacent ray divisors intersect once and nonadjacent ray divisors do not meet. If adjacent primitive rays satisfy $u_{i-1}+u_{i+1}=b_i u_i$, then $D_i^2=-b_i$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-2419559e8348e524" tags="definition"}
What is: $\mathrm{Pic}(X)$ and the divisor class group
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-2419559e8348e524"}
For a toric variety with character lattice $M$, principal divisors give $M\to\bigoplus_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\mathbb ZD_\rho$, $m\mapsto\sum_\rho\langle m,u_\rho\rangle D_\rho$. Its cokernel is $\operatorname{Cl}(X)$. The subgroup represented by Cartier divisors is $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$; for a smooth toric variety, $\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\operatorname{Cl}(X)$.
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-971cee097494589c" tags="unsorted"}
What is $\sigma\dual$?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-971cee097494589c"}
The dual of a cone $\sigma$ is
$$
\sigma^{\vee}:=\left\{u \in M_{\mathbb{R}}:\langle u, v\rangle \geq 0 \forall v \in \sigma\right\}
$$
:::

::: {.front card-id="card-eeb51e03ea304351" tags="unsorted"}
What is the canonical sheaf of a toric variety?
:::

::: {.back card-id="card-eeb51e03ea304351"}
Theorem 8.2.3. For a toric variety $X_{\Sigma}$, the canonical sheaf $\omega_{X_{\Sigma}}$ is given by
$$
\omega_{X_{\Sigma}} \simeq \mathscr{O}_{X_{\Sigma}}\left(-\sum_\rho D_\rho\right) .
$$
Thus $K_{X_{\Sigma}}=-\sum_\rho D_\rho$ is a torus-invariant canonical divisor on $X_{\Sigma}$.

On the dense torus, choose a lattice basis of characters and the invariant rational top form $\omega=(d\chi^{m_1}/\chi^{m_1})\wedge\cdots\wedge(d\chi^{m_n}/\chi^{m_n})$. Along each torus-invariant prime divisor $D_\rho$, it has a simple pole and no zero. Hence $\operatorname{div}(\omega)=-\sum_\rho D_\rho$. On a normal toric variety, the associated rank-one reflexive sheaf is the canonical sheaf.
:::
